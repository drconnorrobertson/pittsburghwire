EPD Electronics

Manufacturing and Engineering · Natrona Heights, Pennsylvania

By Dr. Connor Robertson · Updated October 25, 2026

EPD Electronics in the Pittsburgh area

EPD Electronics appears in EPD Electronics official website under Manufacturing and Engineering. Its listed location is 5 Acee Drive, Natrona Heights, PA 15065. This profile brings the location, website, and available contact information together for readers exploring businesses in Natrona Heights and the Pittsburgh region.

From prototype development to electronics manufacturing

EPD Electronics is an electronics design and manufacturing company with an official contact address at 5 Acee Drive in Natrona Heights. The company’s history says it was founded in 1986 as Electronic Prototype Development and grew from prototyping into design, engineering, manufacturing and assembly. That is historical background published by the operator, not a new opening announcement. The Natrona Heights location places this business in the wider Pittsburgh region, rather than within the City of Pittsburgh.

For a customer evaluating EPD, the useful starting distinction is the maturity of the product. An idea that still needs a circuit design is different from an approved assembly ready for a production quotation. Explain whether the enquiry concerns a new concept, a revision or repeat manufacturing. That gives the company a clearer basis for identifying the appropriate discussion and avoids treating design work as if it were already included in a simple assembly price.

Source: EPD company background.

Published board assembly capabilities

EPD’s PCBA capabilities page lists surface-mount, through-hole and mixed-technology assembly, along with rigid, flexible and rigid-flex board work. It also lists inspection and testing capabilities. These published categories help a buyer identify relevant questions; they do not mean the company has reviewed a specific board, confirmed component availability or approved a design for a particular use.

When preparing an assembly enquiry, identify the intended board revision and whether components will be supplied by the customer or sourced as part of the job. Discuss how substitutions require approval, especially when an older component is difficult to obtain. Keep the assembly quantity separate from an estimate of future demand. A trial run and a recurring order may involve different purchasing assumptions, and those assumptions should be visible in the eventual proposal.

Source: EPD PCBA capabilities.

Testing is a separate conversation from assembly

The official testing page describes work involving test routines, custom fixtures, functional testing and support for older test arrangements. It also discusses design-for-testing assistance. Readers should ask which of those services applies to their own product and what customer information is needed to define the test. A statement that a supplier offers testing does not establish the acceptance criteria for every assembly it produces.

A practical brief should distinguish checking that an assembly was built as specified from demonstrating that a complete product performs as intended. Ask who develops the test procedure, who owns any fixture and which results are retained with a batch. If programming or configuration is part of the work, identify the approved version. Those are project-definition questions, not a claim that EPD includes all such activities automatically or that a directory profile can validate an electronic design.

Source: EPD testing services.

Contact details and a useful first enquiry

EPD’s contact page lists telephone 724-540-5125, extension 117, and the Natrona Heights mailing address. Use the official form or published contact route for current enquiries. Older third-party directories can show other addresses, so readers should rely on the current operator information and confirm delivery or visit arrangements directly. No public walk-in access or immediate production availability is established by a street address alone.

For an initial message, describe the product stage, the type of help required and the next decision you need to make. Before sending confidential drawings, software or a full component list, agree on an appropriate channel and any confidentiality arrangements. Record the specification used for a quotation and ask how later changes would affect the work. This profile’s preparation guidance is intended to make a technical conversation clearer; it is not an independent assessment of quality, a promised response time or a recommendation to bypass the customer’s own engineering review.

When calling the published number, note extension 117 as shown on the official contact page. Ask which contact should receive the project brief so technical questions and commercial questions reach the appropriate person rather than assuming the same enquiry route handles every stage.

Source: EPD contact information.
