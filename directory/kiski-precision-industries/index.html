Kiski Precision Industries

Manufacturing and Engineering · Leechburg, Pennsylvania

By Dr. Connor Robertson · Updated October 25, 2026

Kiski Precision Industries in the Pittsburgh area

Kiski Precision Industries appears in Kiski Precision Industries official website under Manufacturing and Engineering. Its listed location is 531 Hyde Park Road, Leechburg, PA 15656. This profile brings the location, website, and available contact information together for readers exploring businesses in Leechburg and the Pittsburgh region.

A regional contract manufacturer in Allegheny Township

Kiski Precision Industries serves manufacturing customers from 531 Hyde Park Road, Leechburg, PA 15656. Westmoreland County’s company spotlight places the campus in Allegheny Township’s Westmoreland Industrial Park II. The mailing city and the municipality describe different aspects of the same location; this profile does not place the plant inside the City of Pittsburgh.

The county account says the business began in 2005 as Alle-Kiski Industries and adopted the Kiski Precision name in 2021. It identifies President David Holm and describes growth into a wider collection of manufacturing services. That history helps explain why the business belongs in a regional directory: its customers may be looking for components or assemblies that support another company’s finished product, rather than an item displayed in a retail showroom.

Source: Westmoreland County company spotlight.

Machining and supporting production capabilities

Kiski’s machining page describes CNC and manual work, including multi-axis machining, turning and supporting inspection. Its published material list spans steels, aluminum, titanium, nickel alloys and engineering plastics. The company also lists welding, fabrication and other supporting operations. Treat those descriptions as a starting point for a technical conversation, rather than confirmation that a particular drawing has been accepted or a production slot reserved.

Buyers should specify which features drive acceptance and which dimensions allow flexibility. A quotation is easier to compare when it identifies material, quantity, drawing revision, inspection expectations and delivery requirements. If alternative materials or a design adjustment are proposed, ask for the effect on the agreed specification before authorizing the change. These are practical purchasing questions, not claims about a particular customer’s experience with Kiski.

Source: Kiski precision-machining capabilities.

Beyond parts: assembly, wiring and testing

The electromechanical assembly page describes a build-to-print service that can combine manufactured parts, sourced components, mechanical assembly, wiring and testing. It distinguishes customers seeking individual parts from those needing a more complete system build. The page also identifies machine bases and custom enclosures as areas where the company offers its own design work. Readers should confirm where design responsibility begins and ends for their specific project.

For a larger assembly, prepare more than a single component drawing. Discuss the parts list, approved bought-in components, electrical information, test sequence and delivery condition expected at handover. Ask how revisions are communicated while a build is in progress and which documents accompany the finished work. Those details help define a contract manufacturing relationship without assuming that a broad turnkey description automatically includes every engineering or installation task.

An integrated build also makes ownership of customer-supplied information important. Identify which drawings are controlled by the customer, which purchased components can be substituted only with approval, and which test results must be retained for later repeat work. Ask the project contact to distinguish prototype learning from the requirements for a production release. If a trial assembly leads to an improvement, record the approved revision before requesting another batch. These steps create a clearer brief for Kiski or any other prospective contract manufacturer and help keep technical decisions attached to the correct version of the product.

Source: Kiski electromechanical assembly overview.

Enquiries, facility visits and written project scope

The official contact page lists 724-845-2799 and directs ready-to-quote projects toward a dedicated quotation route. General enquiries can be used to discuss capabilities or arrange a prospective facility visit. Confirm any visit in advance. Published pages describe different areas and footprints within the operation, so this profile does not turn the various square-footage figures into a single verified measure of currently available production capacity.

Before choosing a supplier, distinguish a capability discussion from an accepted order. Request a proposal that makes the deliverables, exclusions and required customer inputs explicit. Confirm who will approve a first article or sample, what evidence is needed for acceptance and how any later repeat orders will reference the original specification. These questions are particularly useful when a project crosses several processes, because an unresolved assumption can carry from an early component into the finished assembly.

Source: Kiski contact and enquiry instructions.

Machine bases as a separate project starting point

Kiski publishes Standard, Advanced and Custom configurations for welded machine bases. Its product page distinguishes a pre-engineered configurable base from versions with panels or doors and from a more tailored design. This creates a specific alternative to beginning every enquiry as a completely new machine project. A buyer can ask whether an existing configuration is relevant before commissioning a custom approach.

The page lists choices involving tabletop, feet and finishing, along with project-dependent options. Those lists should be read together, rather than treating the most favorable example from each category as a guaranteed combination. The profile does not assign a load rating or structural suitability to an unreviewed configuration. Discuss the actual equipment, support and access requirements with the responsible professionals and request a proposal for the agreed arrangement.

For an enquiry, separate the support structure from equipment mounted on it and from any electrical or enclosure work. Explain whether the base is being purchased alone or included in an assembled system. Ask which drawings, finishes and interfaces require customer approval before fabrication. A written record of those decisions is particularly helpful when a later machine revision changes the surrounding equipment but the base is expected to remain in service.

Source: Kiski welded machine-base options.
