How to Volunteer for Pittsburgh’s Greenways

City resources explain how residents can propose cleanups, trail improvements, gardens and orchards in Pittsburgh greenways, with separate applications and agreements depending on the work they want to undertake.

By The Pittsburgh Wire October 10, 2026 3 min read

Pittsburgh residents interested in caring for neighborhood green space have several routes through the city's Greenways Stewardship Program. The right starting point depends on the work: a litter cleanup, a proposed trail or a community orchard follows a different process. Current city resources explain how individuals, nonprofits and neighborhood groups can get involved.

The city's greenways engagement page connects prospective stewards with volunteer applications and the City Farms Garden Program. It describes greenways as permanently conserved public spaces that protect tree canopy, support recreation and help reduce heat and landslide risks. For neighbors with an idea, those pages provide a practical starting point before work begins.

Start with the kind of work you want to do

The Greenways Stewardship Program lists activities handled through its volunteer application, including invasive plant removal, litter cleanup, mowing, restoration planting and entrance landscaping. The page links directly to the application, so residents can describe their interest through the city's process.

More substantial improvements require a stewardship agreement. That list includes building trails and footbridges, installing signs or benches, stream restoration and slope stabilization. The city directs people interested in these projects to a separate Department of Public Works intake form. An idea for a new walking connection therefore needs more coordination than a cleanup proposal.

Before applying, write down the location, the intended activity and the people or organization prepared to maintain it. Ask city staff which process fits the project rather than treating a volunteer application as permission for construction. The program page provides greenways@pittsburghpa.gov for questions and suggested improvements.

Gardens and orchards have their own route

The City Farms Garden Program, managed by CitiParks, supports urban agriculture in city-owned parks and greenways. It offers five-year leases and describes technical and material assistance, including fencing and soil nutrient testing, at no cost to participating gardeners.

Its process begins with identifying a potential site and submitting an intake form with the park or greenway name and parcel numbers. Applicants must notify registered community organizations and engage residents through a public meeting. Required application materials include a narrative, site plan, budget, insurance certificate and maintenance plan. The city estimates two to three months from intake to an executed agreement, depending on how quickly materials arrive.

Potential projects include shared community gardens, individual allotments, pollinator plantings and orchards. A group considering one should discuss both growing plans and ongoing responsibilities early, then use the city process to establish whether the proposed site and project can move forward.

Understand the space before planning a project

The city's greenways directory shows how varied these landscapes are. Hazelwood Greenway has trail networks and restoration areas; the Hollows in Perry North is a conservation greenway without formal trails. Fairhaven in Overbrook has a main loop accessible near Belleville and Norva streets. Those differences matter when discussing access, maintenance and community goals.

The directory publishes a 2026 plan for 21 additional greenways and more than 3,000 added acres. That forward-looking statement should not be read as confirmation that every designation or improvement is complete. Residents can use the existing maps and contact city staff about a particular parcel. Stewardship begins with matching an achievable neighborhood idea to the land, the necessary approval and the people ready to care for it.
