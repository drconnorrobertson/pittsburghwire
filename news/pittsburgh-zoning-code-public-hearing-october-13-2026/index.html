Pittsburgh Zoning Hearing Held; Bill 2026-0834 Still Pending

Pittsburgh City Council’s Oct. 13 hearing on a broad zoning rewrite is over, but its official meeting record still lists draft minutes and no posted action.

By The Pittsburgh Wire Updated October 13, 2026 3 min read

Updated October 13, 2026: Pittsburgh City Council’s Committee on Hearings and Policy held its scheduled public hearing on Bill 2026-0834, a broad zoning-code amendment. The City’s meeting record now shows the Oct. 13 agenda as final, but minutes remain in draft and the record says actions and results are not yet available. No adoption or committee vote is confirmed in the public record checked for this update.

The official meeting page lists the Committee on Hearings and Policy meeting at 1:30 p.m. on Oct. 13. It identifies the bill as an agenda item and cautions that minutes have not been finalized, so the public cannot yet use that page to confirm what speakers said or whether members took action.

What the bill proposes

Bill 2026-0834 would change multiple parts of Title Nine, Pittsburgh’s Zoning Code. The official legislation record includes proposed edits to district rules, residential and mixed-use regulations, overlays, planning districts, primary and accessory uses, environmental standards, residential compatibility, development requirements, signs, nonconformities, review procedures and definitions. The City’s zoning-code amendment hub describes the proposal as an initial phase responding to Executive Order 2026-01.

That breadth does not mean every parcel or pending project would change in the same way. The citywide bill is a set of proposed code amendments, not approval of a particular private development. Property owners evaluating a site-specific effect should read the bill and ask City planning staff about the applicable rules.

Downtown map change is part of the proposal

The bill also proposes rezoning a block in the Central Business District from Golden Triangle Subdistrict D, or GT-D, to GT-C. The proposed area is bounded by the Monongahela River, Boulevard of the Allies, Commonwealth Place and Stanwix Street, according to the City’s public notice and map materials. The notice does not tie that boundary change to a named tenant or project.

The text and map amendments are related items within one ordinance, but each has distinct practical implications. Readers should consult the City’s posted map rather than infer the proposed district boundary from a general description.

What happens after the hearing?

The City has not yet posted finalized minutes or an action result for the Oct. 13 meeting. The bill’s public record therefore does not establish whether testimony prompted revisions, whether the committee advanced the measure, or when another vote will occur. The scheduled public hearing itself is not an adoption vote. The City’s process page says the amendment returns to a standing committee for a vote; if advanced, a final Council vote would follow, with the mayor’s signature required for enactment.

Before the hearing, the City listed the session for 1:30 to 5 p.m. at the City-County Building, with in-person and virtual participation. That advance notice described the chance to provide testimony; it does not substitute for the meeting record. Residents seeking the latest status should check the Legistar bill page and the City’s process guide for finalized minutes or subsequent committee and Council entries. As of this update, Bill 2026-0834 remains a proposal with no post-hearing action publicly listed.

Sources for this update were checked October 13, 2026. The Pittsburgh Wire did not conduct interviews for this article.
