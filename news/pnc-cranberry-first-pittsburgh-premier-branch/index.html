PNC Opens First Pittsburgh-Area Premier Branch in Cranberry

PNC has reopened its renovated Cranberry Township branch as the first local site in its Premier Branch program, bringing a new format for clients seeking coordinated banking and investment support.

By The Pittsburgh Wire October 12, 2026 3 min read

PNC has reopened its renovated branch at 1340 Freedom Road in Cranberry Township as the first Pittsburgh-area location in its Premier Branch program. The Oct. 3 reopening adds a new format to the bank’s local network, pairing everyday banking access with dedicated bankers and investment advisers for clients who want financial guidance in one place.

The location is part of PNC’s broader effort to bring its Premier Client model into physical branches. The bank said in its February program announcement that it plans to convert 200 existing branches into Premier Branches through 2027. A local report on the Cranberry unveiling identified the Freedom Road branch as the first in the Pittsburgh area.

A local branch with a wider role

PNC describes Premier Client as an integrated banking and investing experience for individuals and families with more than $100,000 at PNC. The model brings a personal banker and financial adviser together, with the stated aim of connecting clients’ banking, borrowing and investing needs. The branch renovation is the in-person extension of that offering, rather than a separate bank or a new Cranberry development.

For residents, the practical change is the addition of a dedicated service environment at an established branch site. The bank’s announcement says Premier Branch locations will have dedicated Premier Banking and Investment Advisors alongside updated amenities. PNC has not announced in the cited material which additional Pittsburgh-area branches will receive the format or a schedule for local conversions, so Cranberry is the confirmed first step rather than a timeline for a regionwide rollout.

The branch sits along Freedom Road near the township’s commercial corridor and serves a fast-growing suburban area where financial services compete for customers alongside retail, health care and professional offices. Its reopening gives PNC a local place to introduce the coordinated model while continuing standard branch banking at the same address. Customers can check current services and hours through the bank’s Cranberry branch locator before visiting.

The same regional coverage also reported that PNC committed about $25 million to Western Pennsylvania branch renovations in 2026. That figure applies to the broader local program; it is not a disclosed cost for the Cranberry work. PNC’s nationwide branch investment is a separate, larger effort, and the February announcement does not assign a dollar amount to each Premier conversion.

Customers comparing the service model should confirm eligibility and appointment details directly with PNC. The launch announcement describes a target audience and a team structure, but does not publish a Cranberry-specific fee schedule or say every advisory service is included with a deposit account. PNC’s disclosures also distinguish bank deposits from investment products, which can carry market risk. The locator provides a practical starting point for checking the branch’s current contact information before arranging a visit.

What the announcement signals

The project links PNC’s Pittsburgh headquarters to a customer-facing change in its own backyard. The company is using a familiar neighborhood branch to test how a more advice-oriented format fits into its retail network, while its announced national plan makes clear that the concept extends well beyond western Pennsylvania.

For the region, the next measure will be whether PNC names more local branches for conversion and how the model is staffed and used by customers. The company’s February announcement sets a 2027 target for 200 Premier Branches overall; it does not establish a Cranberry-only schedule or guarantee a specific number for the Pittsburgh area. For now, the reopened Freedom Road location is the area’s first confirmed example of the approach.
