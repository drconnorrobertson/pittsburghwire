PIT Opens Pittsburgh's Only Free Outdoor Airport Terrace

The 36,000-square-foot UPMC Terrace lets anyone, ticketed or not, step outside for fresh air and plane views before security, putting Pittsburgh International among a handful of U.S. airports offering the amenity at no cost.

By Megan Strickland September 2, 2026 3 min read

Pittsburgh International Airport has opened its first public outdoor terrace, a 36,000-square-foot gathering space on the arrivals level where anyone can walk outside for fresh air and a view of planes taking off, no boarding pass required. The UPMC Terrace, unveiled at a ribbon-cutting ceremony, puts PIT on a short list of American airports offering pre-security outdoor space free of charge, joining San Francisco's SkyTerrace in a category still rare outside warm-weather hubs like Honolulu and Palm Springs.

The terrace sits near the Welcome Point and the VisitPittsburgh desk, close to Café Conmigo and baggage carousels 5 through 8, in the new landside terminal that opened last November as part of the airport's $1.7 billion terminal modernization. Walking paths, native plants and seating draw on western Pennsylvania's landscape, echoing design choices used throughout the new terminal, from columns modeled on tree branches to signage rendered in the black and gold of the region's sports teams. The space will stay open during regular airport hours year-round, roughly 4 a.m. to midnight, and visitors can eat and drink outside, though smoking is not permitted.

A Front Porch for the Region

Allegheny County Airport Authority CEO Christina Cassotis called the terrace one of the most carefully designed outdoor spaces at any U.S. airport, built to let both travelers and staff experience the character of western Pennsylvania and Allegheny County. County Executive Sara Innamorato struck a similar note at the opening, emphasizing that the space carries no cost of entry and requires no club membership or premium ticket to enjoy.

"You don't have to have club access or fly business class to be able to experience this beautiful space."

Sara Innamorato, Allegheny County Executive

The terrace was funded by UPMC and the Richard King Mellon Foundation, part of a broader push by both organizations to back civic spaces that extend beyond their core missions. James Taylor, UPMC's senior vice president and chief culture, community and talent management officer, described the terrace as a community space first and an airport amenity second, one meant to reinforce Pittsburgh's reputation for innovation and wellbeing to the millions of visitors who pass through the terminal each year.

For businesses inside the terminal, the terrace is already changing how people move through the building. Derek Davies, owner of Café Conmigo, which sits just steps away, said customers had been asking to go outside since the new terminal opened, and he expects the terrace to become a draw for travelers waiting out a delay, greeters killing time before a flight lands, and locals looking for an excuse to linger. Some airport watchers have floated a nostalgic comparison to PIT's AirMall era, when the airport's shopping concourse drew non-ticketed visitors from across the region simply looking for something to do.

What Comes Next

The UPMC Terrace is the first of four outdoor spaces planned for the new terminal. A second pre-security terrace and the Green Spine, a landscaped area in front of the parking garage, are still to come, along with two post-security terraces reserved for ticketed passengers. Timelines for the remaining spaces are still being finalized as the airport authority wraps up final work on a terminal that has already drawn international recognition for its design.

The terrace arrives as PIT positions itself among a small cohort of major airports, including Denver, San Diego and JFK, experimenting with outdoor space as a functional part of terminal design rather than a luxury reserved for airline lounges. For a region that has spent the better part of a decade rebuilding its airport from the ground up, the terrace offers something simpler than a new gate or a faster security line: a place to sit outside, watch a plane climb into the sky over the Allegheny hills, and feel, if only for a few minutes, like there's nowhere else to be.
