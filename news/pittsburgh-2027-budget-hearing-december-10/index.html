Pittsburgh’s 2027 Budget Hearing Is December 10. Here’s What to Watch

City Council will hear the operating and capital proposals together. Find the bills, department review dates, and the next scheduled vote.

By The Pittsburgh Wire November 28, 2026 2 min read

Pittsburgh City Council is scheduled to hold a public hearing on the proposed 2027 city budget on December 10 at 10 a.m. The agenda includes separate bills for the operating budget and the capital budget. The distinction matters for residents trying to follow a city service or a neighborhood project through the vote.

What is on the December 10 agenda?

The operating proposal, Council file 2026-1124, would appropriate about $727.3 million from the general fund for 2027. The bill would also authorize an $8.7 million transfer from the general fund to a pay-as-you-go fund for the capital budget. Those are entries in the proposed legislation. The mayor’s office said in its November presentation that the plan would use $28 million from the rainy-day fund.

The capital proposal, file 2026-1123, covers the 2027 capital budget, the proposed Community Development Program and the 2027 through 2032 Capital Improvement Program. Check the year beside a project when reading the capital document. A project listed in a six-year plan may have funding programmed in a later year. To follow a specific park or street project, record its name, the amount listed for 2027 and the funding source, then compare those details with the adopted version.

Which tax bills are also on the agenda?

The hearing agenda also lists file 2026-1134, which would set the city’s 2027 real estate tax rate, and file 2026-1133, which would set payment, discount and delinquency dates. A separate unearned income tax proposal, file 2026-1052, is listed for the same hearing. Its docket shows it held in committee. If you plan to comment, give the file number so Council can identify which proposal you are addressing.

The rate bill proposes 9.67 mills on both land and buildings, or $9.67 per $1,000 of taxable assessed value. The payment schedule bill proposes a 2% early-payment discount through February 10, with February 26 as the full-payment or first-installment due date. It lists April 30 and July 30 for the next installments. These figures are from the proposed city bills. Check the adopted text and official tax bill before relying on the dates or rate.

Which meeting should residents follow?

Council’s calendar lists department reviews before the public hearing. Parks and Recreation is scheduled for December 1 at 1:30 p.m., Public Works for December 2 at 1:30 p.m. and Mobility and Infrastructure for December 3 at 1:30 p.m. The Office of Management and Budget is scheduled for December 9 at 1:30 p.m. Those sessions are a chance to hear departments explain their proposals. The December 10 hearing agenda brings the budget legislation together in one place.

Council lists a line-item vote on December 15 at 1:30 p.m. and a separate final budget vote at its special regular meeting on December 17 at 10 a.m. Eastern. Check the final vote and adopted bill versions before treating any proposed spending as approved. Check the current clerk’s calendar before attending in person or watching online. The clerk publishes meeting locations and livestream information. If you want to speak at the December 10 hearing, register by 8 a.m. Eastern that morning, two hours before the scheduled start. Use the speaker signup form on the City Clerk’s meeting schedule or call 412-255-2138. Check the hearing listing again before making plans.

How to follow a neighborhood issue through the vote

Start with a specific question. If you want to know whether a park improvement is funded next year, find the project line in the capital materials. If you want to know how a department plans to deliver a recurring service, read the operating proposal and watch that department’s review. Write down the relevant file number and check the legislation page after the hearing for a new version or council action. An agenda shows what is scheduled for consideration. The recorded vote and adopted file show what council approved.
