Scale Expo Brings Makers to North Point Breeze October 27

Innovation Works will connect hardware founders with Pennsylvania manufacturers at Rockwell Park. The free event offers Pittsburgh entrepreneurs a place to discuss the practical work between a prototype and a production run.

By Dr. Connor Robertson October 25, 2026 3 min read

North Point Breeze will host a practical meeting of Pittsburgh’s startup and manufacturing communities on Tuesday, October 27. Innovation Works is bringing Scale Expo to Rockwell Park, where founders developing physical products can meet potential production partners. The organizer’s event calendar lists the gathering from 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. at 7514 Thomas Boulevard. For companies trying to turn an early design into something repeatable, that creates a specific local destination for conversations that often begin with a much broader supplier search.

A meeting focused on production

In its October 20 announcement, Innovation Works says more than 30 Pennsylvania manufacturers will participate and more than 150 founders are expected. Those are the organizer’s advance expectations, rather than a count of completed attendance. The event is intended for hardware, robotics and medical-device startups seeking help with an initial production run or an existing product line. Named participants include Tronix3D, Kiski Precision Industries, EPD Electronics and National Rubber Corporation. Kevin Dowling leads Robotics Factory and Hardware at Innovation Works; the announcement describes the Expo as a way to connect founders with manufacturing resources close to home.

How founders can prepare

The organizer’s registration and exhibitor information says attendance is free for founders and their teams. Advance registration is requested, with on-site registration also permitted. That page identifies the location as North Point Breeze and describes a format built around conversations with producers. Attendees should check the current official listing before leaving, particularly if they need accessibility information, directions or details about a particular exhibitor.

For a founder preparing to attend, a useful starting point is a short description of the part or product, its intended use and the manufacturing question that needs an answer. A discussion about a prototype is different from a request for a repeat order. Bringing drawings that can appropriately be shared, an estimated quantity and a realistic target schedule can help distinguish those needs. This is preparation guidance, not an event requirement or a promise that any supplier will accept a project.

Make the next conversation specific

Founders can also ask how a manufacturer handles design revisions, sample approval and communication after an initial meeting. A written follow-up that identifies the part under discussion and the next decision is more useful than a general request to stay in touch. Before sharing proprietary files, teams should decide what information they are comfortable disclosing and establish any needed confidentiality arrangements directly with the prospective partner. The Expo is an introduction; project scope and commercial terms still need their own careful discussion.

Readers researching the wider local network can explore the Catalyst Connection directory profile and the Pittsburgh Wire business directory. Innovation Works names Catalyst Connection, PGH | MFG and the ARM Institute as event partners. Their presence gives attendees additional organizations to ask about regional resources, without assuming that a particular program or service fits every company. For North Point Breeze, the gathering puts an existing business campus at the center of a tangible Pittsburgh opportunity: helping the people designing products meet the people equipped to make them.
