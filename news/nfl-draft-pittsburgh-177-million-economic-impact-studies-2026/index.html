NFL Draft Delivered $177M Impact, Studies Find

Two independent analyses released Monday put a figure on Pittsburgh's April: $177 million in regional economic impact, and more than $1.5 billion in total economic value once global media exposure is counted.

By The Pittsburgh Wire August 11, 2026 4 min read

Pittsburgh has spent most of the summer trading anecdotes about what the NFL Draft did for the city. Hotel managers had their stories, restaurant owners had theirs, and everyone who stood on the Boulevard of the Allies in late April had a version. On Monday the arithmetic finally caught up. Two independent studies commissioned around the event found the three-day Draft delivered $177 million in economic impact to the Pittsburgh region and generated more than $1.5 billion in economic value once worldwide media exposure is priced in.

The work was split between two firms measuring two very different things. Sportsimpacts handled the ground-level economics: visitor spending, the shape of the crowd, and how the trip changed what people thought of the city. Trajektory took on the harder question, which is what it is worth to have Pittsburgh's rivers and bridges filling television screens for three consecutive nights in April.

What the Visitors Took Home

The Sportsimpacts perception findings are, in some ways, the more interesting half of the report. Eighty-three percent of visitors said they were very satisfied with their trip. Seventy-eight percent rated Pittsburgh an excellent host city for large-scale events. Fifty-one percent said their opinion of the city improved simply by coming here, which is a polite way of saying half the people who showed up arrived carrying an outdated picture in their heads and left with a new one. Three quarters said they expect to be back inside five years.

The visibility, reputation and momentum generated over the past two years have fundamentally elevated how Pittsburgh is perceived around the world.

Jerad Bachar, President and CEO, VisitPittsburgh

Bachar framed the result as two legacies rather than one. The first was immediate: record visitation, a packed Downtown, and a weekend that people will describe for years. The second, he argued, is only starting to pay out, and it lives in the reputational ledger rather than the cash register.

The Exposure Ledger

Trajektory's numbers give that argument some weight. The firm valued Draft-week publicity at $1.41 billion, added $27 million in owned and earned social media value and $4.74 million in direct in-venue value, and counted 209 million in-venue impressions on top of the broadcast totals. Pittsburgh generated more than twice the impressions and follower growth of any previous Draft host city. Social post value ran 69 percent above the three-year host average, and the Steelers posted the highest Draft-week social engagement of any club in the league. None of that could have been bought with an advertising budget of any plausible size.

Governor Josh Shapiro, whose administration timed a package of Downtown improvements to the Draft calendar, called the report evidence that investing in tourism and major events drives real economic momentum, and said the Commonwealth intends to keep investing in Downtown Pittsburgh. Peter O'Reilly, the NFL's executive vice president for club business, international and league events, said Pittsburgh set a new standard for the Draft and surpassed expectations for fans, prospects and partners alike.

What It Means for Pittsburgh

The $177 million has already been spent and largely dispersed. What remains is harder to deposit and more valuable: a redesigned Market Square, a finished Arts Landing, a Downtown that got scrubbed and lit and planted on a hard deadline, and a global audience that now associates all of it with a city many of them had never seriously considered. Convention bookings, site-selector visits and relocation decisions run on exactly that kind of impression, and they run on a lag of years rather than months. Pittsburgh did not merely host a football event in April. It ran a three-day commercial for itself in front of 55 million people, and the returns on that are only beginning to be counted.
