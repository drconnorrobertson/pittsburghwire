Pittsburgh Pottery Classes: Three Wheel Experiences to Compare

Compare a Carnegie group session, Kiln-N-Time’s individual lessons and Union Project’s beginner workshops by price, finishing costs and booking rules.

By The Pittsburgh Wire October 13, 2026 3 min read

Trying a pottery wheel once and enrolling in a longer ceramics course are different commitments. Pittsburgh-area beginners can compare a short group session, an individually taught lesson and a community workshop before choosing where to start. The advertised class price is only part of the comparison: check whether finishing the pieces costs extra and when you must return to collect them.

Our lead editorial selection is Pittsburgh Pottery for its clear separation of a one-day wheel session from optional finishing charges. Kiln-N-Time and Union Project offer alternatives with different instruction and booking arrangements. Official information was checked October 13, 2026. These are existing programs, and this comparison is an editorial selection rather than an independent award or a report of personal testing. We have not measured query volume for this category.

1. Pittsburgh Pottery: price the finished piece separately

Pittsburgh Pottery’s one-day weekend session is listed at $60. It lasts 2.5 hours, welcomes all skill levels and provides clay and tools. After a demonstration, participants can try making their own work.

The key distinction is what happens afterward. Keeping a piece adds a published $10 per piece for the studio’s potters to trim, glaze and fire it. The page gives a pickup expectation of two weeks after class. That explicit explanation of the session and finishing costs earns our lead position for readers comparing a first experience.

Classes take place at 309 East Main Street, Carnegie, PA 15106. Contact 412-440-3024 or PittsburghPottery@gmail.com. Changes and cancellations must be made at least 48 hours before the class, according to the posted policy. Check the selected date and terms before paying; a changing ticket counter is not a guarantee of availability. Our Pittsburgh Pottery profile links the session information.

2. Kiln-N-Time: book a wheel lesson in advance

Kiln-N-Time advertises one-on-one wheel instruction at 3801 Penn Avenue, Pittsburgh, PA 15201. Adult lessons are listed at $90 for one hour or $180 for two hours, per person. The page requires online booking and a $50 nonrefundable deposit to confirm a reservation.

Do not confuse the studio’s general walk-in pottery-painting access with wheel lessons: the latter must be scheduled. Its policy allows one rescheduling and warns that another change forfeits the deposit. It also asks participants to arrive 10–15 minutes early and bring their own towel.

Read the clothing, footwear, hair and nail requirements before booking. The studio publishes a limited collection period for finished work, so confirm when it begins and when your pieces will be ready. Call 412-687-0383 with questions about suitability or the collection arrangements.

3. Union Project: compare a workshop with open studio

Union Project’s Wheel 101 description is aimed at beginners aged 16 and up. It teaches centering and making bowls and cylinders, with clay, tools, glaze and firing included. Staff finish the work, and participants receive an email when it is ready for pickup a few weeks later.

The October 17 workshop listing publishes $55, $65 and $75 payment levels, distinguishing a discounted option, standard fee and a level supporting other participants. Confirm the current registration status or request a waitlist place; the listing does not guarantee a seat.

Union Project is at 801 North Negley Avenue, Pittsburgh, PA 15206, with 412-363-4550 for contact. Use its calendar to distinguish beginner workshops from open-studio sessions. Check experience requirements, the exact event date and collection terms for the session you choose.
