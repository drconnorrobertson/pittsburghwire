5 Pittsburgh Print and Sign Shops for Business Projects

Compare a South Side walk-in printer, North Hills commercial print services and Pittsburgh sign makers for storefronts, vehicle graphics, banners and more before requesting a quote.

By The Pittsburgh Wire October 11, 2026 3 min read

Businesses planning a new storefront, event, vehicle wrap or print run often need different vendors for different jobs. A walk-in printer may be the simplest fit for a short document run, while permanent building signs can involve surveys, permits, fabrication and installation. This guide compares five Pittsburgh-area shops by the services and workflows they publish. The list is an editorial comparison, not a paid ranking or a report based on facility visits. Our lead business-print option is KDC because its site documents digital and offset printing alongside design, finishing, direct mail and web-to-print. The right choice still depends on the project.

1. KDC for a broad commercial print program

King Digital & Commercial Offset Printing (KDC) is at 2100 Babcock Blvd. in Pittsburgh. The company lists high-speed digital and commercial offset printing, signage and graphics, in-house design, finishing, direct mail and custom web-to-print. It also identifies itself as a Women’s Business Enterprise certified by WBENC, a practical point for organizations tracking supplier-diversity requirements. KDC is the guide’s lead for businesses coordinating several print and communication needs through one vendor; request a project quote to confirm specifications and turnaround.

2. Copies at Carson for walk-in and self-serve work

Copies at Carson operates at 1315 E. Carson St. on Pittsburgh’s South Side. Its site describes walk-in and online service, self-serve equipment, and staff support for design, printing and finishing. The shop lists black-and-white and full-color work, from small to extra-large formats, and posts weekday business hours of 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. That makes it a useful starting point when a business or individual needs to discuss a job in person or handle a smaller run; confirm current equipment availability before traveling.

3. Krohmaly’s Printing for East End signs and graphics

Krohmaly’s Printing, at 7425 Washington Ave. in Pittsburgh, publishes a wide-format sign menu: banners, indoor and outdoor signs, vinyl and vehicle lettering, ADA signs, wall murals, illuminated signs and installation. Customers can submit project details and upload artwork through its quote form. That published range is useful for a company comparing temporary event banners with more durable building or parking-lot signage. The shop asks customers to contact it for custom needs rather than posting standard prices.

4. AdVision Signs for storefront and vehicle graphics

AdVision Signs is at 4735 Campbells Run Road and posts weekday hours of 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Its listed work includes storefront and dimensional signs, channel letters, monument signs, ADA signage, banners, vehicle graphics, tradeshow displays and printed products. Businesses choosing between a permanent exterior sign and mobile fleet graphics can use its service list to scope the discussion before asking for a quote. The company’s about page says its Pittsburgh-based shop was established in 1994.

5. Accel Sign Group for design, permits and installation

Accel Sign Group, at 5600 Harrison St. in Pittsburgh, describes a full project path that includes site surveys, design, permit acquisition, fabrication, installation, maintenance and repair. It lists LED retrofits and electronic message centers among its services and says it works locally, regionally and nationally. This is a practical option to compare for a multi-location program or a project where approvals and installation matter as much as the sign itself. Contact the office to discuss a site and confirm the scope.

Questions to ask before choosing

Send each shop the same brief: finished dimensions, quantity, material, indoor or outdoor use, artwork status, deadline, delivery or installation needs, and whether permits are involved. Ask for a proof, the full price including setup and installation, and an estimated production schedule. For a one-time document run, walk-in access may matter most; for building identity, fabrication and installation experience may be more relevant. These five providers publish enough detail to make an informed first call, but the written estimate and project requirements should decide the final match.
