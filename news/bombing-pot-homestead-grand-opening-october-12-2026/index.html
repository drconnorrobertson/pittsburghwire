Bombing Pot Sets Oct. 12 Grand Opening in Homestead

The Waterfront lists a Monday celebration for its Korean barbecue, hot pot and sushi restaurant, with a noon ribbon cutting and a limited-time half-lobster offer. Here are the confirmed event details and what diners should check before visiting.

By The Pittsburgh Wire October 11, 2026 3 min read

Bombing Pot is scheduled to mark its grand opening at The Waterfront in West Homestead on Monday, Oct. 12, giving Pittsburgh-area diners a new option built around Korean barbecue, hot pot and made-to-order sushi. The shopping center’s event listing sets the celebration from noon to 10 p.m. and names a noon ribbon-cutting ceremony. The restaurant’s tenant page describes an all-you-can-eat format and posts the location at 245 West Bridge St.

A Monday opening event with a limited offer

The Waterfront says the grand-opening celebration will include a free half lobster for every customer, while supplies last. The offer is first-come, first-served, so the page advises arriving early. It does not provide additional eligibility terms or a quantity limit; diners with questions can call the restaurant at 412-800-7888, the number posted in its directory listing. The event hours refer to Monday’s celebration and should not be confused with the shopping center’s general operating schedule.

The celebration gives the restaurant a defined first public milestone, but it is not a multi-day festival or a reservation notice: the published details identify one day, one time window and the ribbon cutting. Anyone planning to attend for the lobster offer should verify availability directly, since the host says supplies may run out. The Waterfront lists the restaurant address as 245 West Bridge St., West Homestead, Pennsylvania 15120.

What Bombing Pot says it serves

The tenant listing positions Bombing Pot as an all-you-can-eat restaurant specializing in Korean barbecue, hot pot and freshly made-to-order sushi. That mix gives groups more than one way to organize a meal: grill-focused dining, broth-based hot pot, or sushi prepared to order. The public listing does not show a full menu or prices, so prospective guests should check with the restaurant rather than rely on menu figures posted by third-party sites.

The same listing publishes a regular weekly schedule: noon to 10:30 p.m. Monday through Thursday, noon to 11 p.m. Friday and Saturday, and noon to 10:30 p.m. Sunday. It identifies weekday lunch as noon to 3 p.m. and Saturday and Sunday as all-day dinner. Because the restaurant is celebrating its opening on Oct. 12, readers should confirm the schedule before making a special trip, especially around the event.

A new dining option for The Waterfront

Bombing Pot’s arrival adds another group-dining format to the Homestead retail and restaurant district along the Monongahela River. Its advertised menu concept may suit parties looking for a longer, shared-table meal, while the opening event offers a specific date for neighbors to visit and see the space. Those who prefer quieter first visits can wait until after the celebration and use the restaurant’s posted phone number to ask about hours, menu availability and how the all-you-can-eat service works.

The listing does not explain whether every menu item is included in one price or how long a table may dine, details diners may want to clarify for all-you-can-eat service. Ask about those policies and food allergies before ordering. For the most reliable details, check The Waterfront’s Oct. 12 event page and Bombing Pot’s official tenant listing before heading out. The offer is time-limited and supply-dependent; the regular schedule and menu details may change as the restaurant begins service.
