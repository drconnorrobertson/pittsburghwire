Free GED Classes in Pittsburgh: How to Get Started

Literacy Pittsburgh and its CCAC Homewood-Brushton partnership offer free preparation routes. Here is how orientation, class placement, tutoring waitlists and possible testing-fee help fit into the enrollment process.

By The Pittsburgh Wire October 13, 2026 3 min read

Pittsburgh adults working toward a high school equivalency credential have free preparation options through Literacy Pittsburgh, including a partnership at CCAC’s Homewood-Brushton Center. The important distinction is between asking to enroll, receiving a class placement and registering for the credential examination itself.

This guide explains existing services using official information checked October 13, 2026. It does not announce a new program or promise an immediate opening. Applicants should confirm the current schedule, eligibility and any testing assistance directly before making plans around work, transportation or family responsibilities.

Start with Literacy Pittsburgh’s intake process

Literacy Pittsburgh’s student information page says its programs are free and begins the enrollment process with new-student orientation. Staff discuss the applicant’s goals and arrange an assessment of current skills before developing a learning plan.

The page lists adult classes or tutoring for residents of Allegheny, Beaver, Butler, Washington and Westmoreland counties. However, some other enrollment pages still describe a narrower Allegheny-and-Beaver service area. Residents outside those two counties should ask which specific program currently serves their address. The general information page describes a documented exception for certain 17-year-olds; younger applicants should discuss that requirement with staff.

Online study requires internet access and a computer or mobile device. Applicants needing help with those resources are encouraged to tell the organization. Its office is listed at 411 Seventh Avenue, Suite 525, Downtown, and the main phone number is 412-393-7600. Confirm the actual teaching location rather than assuming every class meets at the office.

Compare class and tutoring commitments

The organization’s GED preparation page describes online and in-person learning, with group and individual options. Instruction covers math, language arts, social studies and science, alongside practice tests and test-taking strategies. The program prepares students for GED or HiSET examinations.

Its student FAQ describes classes meeting twice weekly for two hours, with morning, afternoon and evening schedules. Published quarterly starts fall in January, April, July and October, but placement depends on level and availability. Being in a start month does not establish that a seat is open.

Tutoring also requires a regular time commitment. The FAQ describes a waitlist and says matching commonly takes two to three months, with shorter or longer waits possible. Meetings take place in public or online rather than in the student’s home. In-person childcare is not provided. Ask about the attendance policy before choosing a format.

Check the Homewood-Brushton partnership

CCAC’s GED information page lists free, in-person preparation with Literacy Pittsburgh at the Homewood-Brushton Center, 701 North Homewood Avenue. Interested applicants are directed to a waitlist form linked on that page. For questions, CCAC lists 412-371-1600 and CCAC-HBC@ccac.edu.

The page describes a pretest, subject instruction and practice-test preparation. It also says eligible participants receive an examination-fee voucher. Separately, Literacy Pittsburgh says its GED students can apply for a scholarship covering testing fees. Ask which assistance applies to your enrollment; an application is not confirmation that a particular fee has been waived.

Before submitting an intake request, write down your available days, preferred learning format, transportation needs and credential goal. Keep the response from the enrollment team and confirm the next required step. Free preparation can make returning to education more accessible, while a realistic schedule helps turn an inquiry into sustained participation.
