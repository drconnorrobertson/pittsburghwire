Local Authors: Read Pittsburgh Bookstore Submission Policies

Riverstone, The Tiny Bookstore and City Books publish different stocking and event rules. Prepare the title, distribution details and requested materials before approaching a shop.

By Dr. Connor Robertson November 8, 2026 4 min read

A bookstore supporting local readers does not automatically accept every locally written book. Stocking, consignment, direct sales and event proposals are different business arrangements. Pittsburgh-area authors can save time by reading the relevant policy before sending copies or asking staff to review a manuscript. The published route should determine the enquiry, rather than a general assumption about independent shops.

This comparison was checked November 8, 2026 against operator pages from Riverstone Books, The Tiny Bookstore and City Books. It does not submit work, arrange representation or promise placement. An author should keep the ISBN, publisher, distributor, format and publication date together before deciding which policy the book fits.

Riverstone: stocking and event proposals are separate

Riverstone’s author policy generally excludes self-published and print-on-demand books and says it does not accept ordinary consignment stock. It describes limited exceptions for regional nonfiction and tells authors not to drop off or mail books for review. Read the full policy before assuming an exception applies to a particular title.

The separate event guidance discusses proposals, publisher relationships and possible panel formats. An event route should not be interpreted as blanket acceptance for regular shelf stock. Explain the proposed format and audience, then let the store decide whether to pursue it. A form submission is not an approved event, and a general statement that panels exist does not establish an available place.

The Tiny Bookstore: confirm the current program option

The Tiny Bookstore’s Authors Select page describes a route for independently published work, requesting information before copies arrive and distinguishing direct inventory sales from affiliate purchases. However, the narrative about a basic no-fee route sits beside a form listing several tiers, including paid options. Confirm the chosen route and written terms before shipping anything.

The page also mentions a Black Friday relaunch without clearly establishing a year. This report does not present that text as a confirmed November 2026 launch. Ask the shop which service is currently operating. Its broader website says online and curbside only, another reason not to infer that an author can simply walk in and make an unarranged pitch.

City Books: use the dedicated author page

City Books’ author instructions prioritize used and remaindered inventory and set conditions for regional new-book pitches. They describe Ingram ordering, returnability and trade terms, and exclude consignment or direct author sales. The page requests specified review materials and rejects in-person or telephone pitches. Check those instructions rather than relying on a broader description of the shop’s literary interests.

The same page says the shop no longer holds evening on-site events, while allowing possible weekend table signings for authors whose books it carries. That is a conditional possibility, not a booking offer. It also illustrates why a specific policy page can be more useful than an old article describing a store as an event venue.

Prepare a proposal that answers the business question

A concise submission should make the title identifiable and explain how the store could obtain it. If the operator asks about audience or promotion, answer with concrete information rather than a forecast presented as a certainty. Do not claim guaranteed sales or a confirmed audience that has not committed. Use the requested channel and materials; sending extra copies against instructions does not create an obligation to read or return them.

Keep stocking, a signing and a ticketed presentation distinct in your own records. A bookseller might consider one and decline another. If the shop asks for clarification, respond to that point rather than interpreting initial interest as approval. Preserve the terms of any arrangement you eventually agree to, including who supplies inventory and what happens to unsold copies.

Readers looking to attend an existing program can use the author-event attendance guide. Anyone simply buying a title should use the ordering comparison. Those are separate reader needs, and neither replaces an author’s obligation to follow the shop’s own submission policy.
