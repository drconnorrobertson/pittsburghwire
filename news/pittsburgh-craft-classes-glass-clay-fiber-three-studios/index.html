3 Pittsburgh Places to Try Glass, Clay and Craft Classes

Compare beginner options at Pittsburgh Glass Center, Union Project and Contemporary Craft, with practical details about materials, ages and registration to help you choose a first hands-on workshop.

By The Pittsburgh Wire October 9, 2026 3 min read

Trying a new craft is easier when the first decision is the material: glass, clay, fiber, metal or wood. These three Pittsburgh arts organizations provide different routes into hands-on learning, from a brief glassmaking experience to a multi-week ceramics course. This editorial selection focuses on published beginner options and practical planning details, rather than ranking studios or claiming a complete survey. Check each provider's registration page for current dates, available seats, age requirements and prices before booking.

1. Pittsburgh Glass Center: start with a short glass experience

At 5472 Penn Avenue, Pittsburgh Glass Center's class program ranges from short Make-It-Now experiences to multi-week courses and summer intensives. Its class filters separate beginner offerings from intermediate and advanced instruction and distinguish studios such as hot glass, kiln work and the flame shop. That makes the first task choosing both the technique and the amount of time you want to spend learning it.

The center's FAQ says Make-It-Now welcomes participants ages five and older, with children under 14 accompanied by an adult. It asks students to wear flat, closed-toe shoes and natural-fiber clothing and to tie back long hair. A class is separate from a self-guided visit: the center also says visitors can browse the gallery and watch glassblowing during open hours without a class reservation. For a family outing, compare the specific activity's age limit and duration before registering everyone together.

2. Union Project: clay workshops and longer ceramics courses

Union Project, at 801 North Negley Avenue, offers short workshops, multi-week classes and family-friendly ceramics programming throughout the year. Its recurring options include Wheel 101, teen and tween opportunities and courses ranging from basic to advanced. Materials and supplies are included in the class price, and outside clay and glazes are not permitted. For someone trying pottery for the first time, that removes one shopping task before the first session.

The organization offers payment levels on selected programs, with participants choosing the appropriate level without an application. Its published general age policy requires students to be at least 16 to attend without a parent, or at least 14 with a parent, unless an offering states otherwise. Read the individual description carefully when choosing a family workshop. The class page also explains cancellation and makeup policies, useful details when deciding between a single visit and a course requiring several weeks of attendance.

3. Contemporary Craft: explore several materials in Lawrenceville

Contemporary Craft's studios offer workshops in metals and jewelry, blacksmithing, wood, weaving, fibers, paper and book arts, with instruction ranging from beginner-friendly to more advanced work. Its Timmons Studios building is at 5688 Butler Street in Lawrenceville. Confirm the location in your registration details, since the organization's campus includes more than one building.

The published October workshop calendar illustrates the range, listing woodworking, weaving, natural dyeing and bookbinding alongside other techniques. A listing does not guarantee an available seat, so open the individual workshop page for prerequisites, materials information and enrollment status. Start with a material you are curious about and a schedule you can keep. Across these organizations, a first workshop can become a practical introduction to Pittsburgh's working arts community, with teaching studios offering a next step when a short experiment turns into a lasting interest.
