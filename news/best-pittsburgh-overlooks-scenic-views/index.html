The Best Pittsburgh Overlooks and Scenic Views

From Grandview Avenue to West End Overlook Park and the North Shore riverfront, these are the viewpoints that show off Pittsburgh's rivers, bridges and skyline.

By The Pittsburgh Wire Staff September 24, 2026 5 min read

Pittsburgh is a city of hills, rivers and bridges, and that geography produces some of the most striking urban views in the country. You do not need a special ticket to see most of them. Many of the best overlooks are public parks, sidewalks and pull-offs that locals have been visiting for generations. This guide rounds up the viewpoints worth your time, what each one shows you, and how to pair them with a visit to the surrounding neighborhood.

Grandview Avenue, Mount Washington

The classic Pittsburgh view is the one from Grandview Avenue on Mount Washington. A string of overlook platforms along the street looks straight down at the Point, where the Allegheny and Monongahela rivers meet to form the Ohio, with the Downtown skyline, the stadiums on the North Shore and a lineup of bridges all in view. The view is famous enough that it regularly appears on national lists of the best city views in America.

The easiest and most memorable way to get there is to ride one of the city's historic inclines up from West Carson Street. See our guide to Pittsburgh's inclines for details on both.

Emerald View Park

Grandview Avenue sits within the larger Emerald View Park, a network of green hillsides and trails wrapping around Mount Washington and Duquesne Heights. The trails offer quieter, wooded views that feel far removed from the crowds on the main overlooks. The Mount Washington Community Development Corporation has worked for years to build and maintain the park's trail system, and it is a good resource for trail information.

West End Overlook Park

West End Overlook Park, perched above the West End neighborhood, looks east toward the Point and Downtown from a different angle than Mount Washington. From here, the rivers spread out below and the skyline sits in the middle distance, which many photographers prefer for sunrise shots. It is generally less crowded than Grandview Avenue, making it a good option if you want a calmer visit.

Point State Park

Not every great view comes from above. At Point State Park in Downtown, you stand at water level at the tip of the Golden Triangle, with the fountain behind you and the rivers on either side. Looking back up at Mount Washington from the Point is its own kind of overlook, and it is the best place to understand how the city's three rivers fit together. The park is part of the state park system, and details are available from the Pennsylvania Department of Conservation and Natural Resources.

North Shore Riverfront

The riverfront trail on the North Side offers one of the best ground-level skyline views in the city. Walking between the stadiums and along the Allegheny River, you look straight across the water at Downtown, with the three sister bridges (the Roberto Clemente, Andy Warhol and Rachel Carson bridges) lined up along the river. It is especially good in the evening, when the Downtown towers light up and reflect off the water.

Schenley Park

In Oakland, Schenley Park has several spots where the landscape opens up. The area near Schenley Plaza and the Phipps Conservatory gives views across the park and toward the Cathedral of Learning, while paths and roads through the park reveal wooded hollows and the ravines that define so much of Pittsburgh's terrain. The Pittsburgh Parks Conservancy maintains information on the city's major parks and their trails.

Riverview Park

Riverview Park, on the North Side, is home to the Allegheny Observatory and a hilly, wooded landscape with glimpses of the Ohio River valley. It is more of a hiking destination than a single viewpoint, but that makes it a good choice if you want to combine exercise with a look at the city's topography.

Neighborhood Street Views

Some of Pittsburgh's most memorable views are not in parks at all. Hillside neighborhoods like Polish Hill and the Hill District have streets and public stairways that suddenly open up to reveal the Strip District, the Allegheny River or the Downtown skyline. Wandering these neighborhoods on foot, with respect for the residents who live there, is one of the best ways to discover views that never make the postcards.

Tips for Visiting Pittsburgh Overlooks

  • Time it right. Sunrise and sunset produce the best light. The blue hour after sunset, when the buildings are lit but the sky still has color, is a local favorite for photos.
  • Check the weather. Pittsburgh sees plenty of cloudy days. A clear day after rain often produces the sharpest views.
  • Plan for parking. Grandview Avenue parking fills quickly on busy evenings. Consider the inclines or public transit.
  • Dress for the wind. Hilltop overlooks can be noticeably cooler and windier than the streets below.
  • Be a good neighbor. Many viewpoints are in residential areas. Keep noise down and do not block driveways.

Pairing Views With a Neighborhood Visit

Overlooks are best as part of a larger outing. After Grandview Avenue, head down the incline to Station Square and across to the South Side. After the North Shore riverfront, explore the museums and restaurants of the North Side. After Schenley Park, walk into the heart of Oakland. For more ideas, browse our neighborhood guides for Mount Washington, the North Shore and Downtown.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most famous view in Pittsburgh?

The view of Downtown and the Point from Grandview Avenue on Mount Washington is the most famous. It shows the three rivers, the skyline, the stadiums and many of the city's bridges at once.

Are Pittsburgh's overlooks free to visit?

Yes. The overlooks on Grandview Avenue, West End Overlook Park, Point State Park and the city parks are public spaces and free to visit. The inclines that lead to Mount Washington charge a fare.

What is the best time of day for photos?

Sunset and the blue hour just after sunset are the most popular times. West End Overlook Park is a favorite for sunrise because it faces east toward Downtown.

Is there a less crowded alternative to Mount Washington?

West End Overlook Park and the trails of Emerald View Park are usually quieter than the main Grandview Avenue platforms.
