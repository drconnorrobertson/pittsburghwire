PRT Approves Bus Line Refresh for August 2027. Here’s How to Check Your Route

Pittsburgh Regional Transit has approved a revised bus network. Riders can compare routes now and watch for final timetables before changing their daily trips.

By The Pittsburgh Wire November 28, 2026 2 min read

Pittsburgh Regional Transit’s board approved its Bus Line Refresh on November 20, setting up a regionwide bus network change planned for August 2027. For riders, the immediate task is to check what the approved network could mean for a familiar trip. Keep using the agency’s current schedules for travel today, and check again when PRT posts launch timetables.

What the network decision changes

PRT expects 19 routes to run at intervals of 20 minutes or better, up from 11. It expects 40 routes at intervals of 30 minutes or better, up from 27. The 19 are part of the broader frequency picture. Adding the two totals would overstate the number of routes. Those figures describe the network as a whole, so look up the days and hours that matter for your specific trip before counting on a shorter wait.

The agency says 99.4% of current riders would still have transit within a quarter mile of their current bus stop. That measures proximity. To see whether a trip still works for you, check which direction the nearby route travels, where you can transfer and whether it runs when you need it. A stop close to the old one can still mean a different walk or a different connection.

How to compare your trip before the switch

  1. Write down the stops you actually use. Save the boarding and exit locations for both directions, including the nearest intersection. Record the weekday and time you usually travel. A stop on the opposite side of a busy road can change the walking portion of a trip.
  2. Find the route in the network preview. Use the route comparison resources on Engage PRT’s Bus Line Refresh project page. Check that the map displays the refresh network. Use it to examine the proposed path and connections, then use live schedules for current travel.
  3. Check the whole trip. If the new network calls for a transfer, trace the walk between stops and check where you would wait. Compare the return trip separately. Evening, weekend and early-shift riders should pay particular attention to first and last buses once final timetables are available.

What to watch before August 2027

PRT says it will explain changes to riders ahead of implementation. Route maps can help you identify a likely change now. A launch timetable is what you will need to plan an arrival time, verify a connection or find the last bus home. If you receive a route preview at work or school, save the route number and check it again when the agency publishes its final rider materials.

The approval followed multiple rounds of public input. PRT says the latest formal comment period drew about 1,900 comments and that it scaled back an earlier, broader redesign in response to feedback. The next milestone to watch is the release of rider materials and launch schedules. If you rely on a timed transfer or a stop with accessible boarding, those details are worth checking directly before you change your routine.

Sources PRT’s board approval announcement and the agency’s project and route resources.
