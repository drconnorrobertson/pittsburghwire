D-BAT Cranberry Prepares Kensinger Drive Training Facility

The baseball and softball academy lists a Cranberry Township address and describes planned cage, lesson and membership options. Its official website still says coming soon, with no confirmed opening date or hours.

By The Pittsburgh Wire October 13, 2026 3 min read

D-BAT Cranberry is preparing a baseball and softball training facility at 250 West Kensinger Drive, Suite 200, in Cranberry Township. The business appears on the township’s October 12 update of its “Just Opened or Ready to Open” list, but the operator’s own website still describes the location as coming soon.

That distinction matters for families considering winter practice, lessons or a membership. As checked October 13, 2026, the official Cranberry website does not publish an opening day, operating hours or a local phone number. The project should be treated as forthcoming rather than a confirmed walk-in destination.

A new training address in Cranberry

The operator and Cranberry Township’s business update identify the same Kensinger Drive suite. The municipal list combines businesses that have opened with those ready to open; inclusion alone does not establish which stage applies to an individual tenant.

D-BAT’s local site describes an indoor, climate-controlled facility with baseball and softball cages, lessons, camps and clinics, a pro shop and birthday-party options. Those descriptions outline the intended service mix. They do not establish that every offering has a date or an available reservation.

For Pittsburgh-area players north of the city, the useful next step is checking the local site for an operating announcement before travelling. A brand’s national locations or another D-BAT facility’s schedule should not be substituted for Cranberry’s own information.

Pitching-machine practice differs from cage rental

The Cranberry cage information page describes automated machines using real baseballs and supporting slow-pitch and fast-pitch softball. It says machine lanes are first come, first served rather than reserved in advance, with access through a membership or a purchase of swings.

Separately, the page describes cage rentals in half-hour increments and explicitly says those rentals do not include batting machines. Listed equipment includes baseballs or softballs, a batting tee and an L-screen. Families or teams should confirm which format they are selecting instead of assuming a cage reservation includes automated pitching.

Ask about suitable machine settings, equipment requirements, supervision and access for the player’s age before planning a visit. Confirm current capacity and the complete charge in the local booking process once operations and reservations are announced.

Read membership and lesson details before committing

The membership page advertises introductory Gold and founders Platinum plans. Published benefits differ in daily pitching-machine tokens and discounts on lessons, camps, clinics and merchandise. Both descriptions specify individual use, an important distinction for households with multiple players.

Promotional plan names and a posted monthly amount do not explain every contractual condition. Before purchasing, confirm the current price, start date, billing cycle, cancellation terms and whether any introductory rate changes later. Compare the expected visits with paying for the particular service you need.

The local lessons page also labels its coaching staff as coming soon. Wait for specific instructor and appointment information before choosing a lesson. A family can prepare questions about a player’s goals now without treating an unfinished staff listing as a confirmed lesson roster.

The facility’s next useful milestone will be a clear opening announcement with hours and bookable local services. Until then, the verified address and published service distinctions offer a planning starting point, while the opening status remains forthcoming.
