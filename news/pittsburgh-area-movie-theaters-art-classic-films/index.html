3 Pittsburgh-Area Theaters for Art and Classic Films

Compare Row House Lawrenceville, Row House Hollywood in Dormont and the Harris Theater Downtown, with screening formats, ticket policies and accessibility details to check before planning a movie night.

By The Pittsburgh Wire October 10, 2026 3 min read

A movie night around Pittsburgh can start with a restored classic, a foreign film or a screening that pairs cinema with live performance. Three local venues offer different settings for that outing: a small Lawrenceville room, a larger Dormont destination and an art-film theater in the Cultural District. This editorial selection compares their published programming and visit details rather than ranking them. Information was checked October 10; consult the individual screening page for the current price, format, availability and any special-event rules.

1. Row House Lawrenceville: a small room on Butler Street

Row House Lawrenceville is at 4115 Butler Street. Its rental information describes an 86-seat theater, making it the intimate option among these three venues. The cinema's programming includes older films, classics and art films. Start with the current calendar, then select the exact date and seat rather than assuming that a title plays every evening.

The published policies say all seats are assigned and seating usually begins 20 minutes before showtime. Guests arriving more than five minutes late may be refused admission. Online purchases carry a nonrefundable processing fee; buying in person avoids that fee. Regular screenings can be exchanged with at least 48 hours' notice, while events and one-time screenings have different restrictions. Its accessibility page lists two wheelchair locations with companion seats, audio amplification for all screenings and closed-caption devices for select screenings. Check the showtime listing for caption availability and contact staff about accommodations before booking.

2. Row House Hollywood: large-format films in Dormont

Row House Hollywood, at 1449 Potomac Avenue in Dormont, offers a different scale. Its MegaScreen page describes a 389-seat main auditorium with a balcony, digital projection and 35mm and 70mm film capabilities. Those capabilities do not mean every showing uses a film print: inspect the format attached to the particular screening.

The theater's film-program guide separates MegaScreen and Underground presentations and includes new releases, analog formats and special series. Its October calendar lists Frankenstein with the Bach Choir of Pittsburgh on October 24 and 25, an option for people interested in a live-score event. Confirm the auditorium, date and ticket conditions before purchasing. Row House operates both Lawrenceville and Hollywood, so checking the venue name is especially useful when following a film across the company's website. A ticket for one location should not be assumed to work at the other.

3. Harris Theater: art films in the Cultural District

The Pittsburgh Cultural Trust's Harris Theater is at 809 Liberty Avenue Downtown. The Trust describes a 194-seat venue with a restored balcony and programming that spans contemporary, foreign and classic films. Tickets are available online or at the door at screening time, subject to availability.

Its October schedule includes Kwaidan through October 13 and Vampyr with a live score by the Andrew Alden Ensemble on October 11. Use the event page to choose the actual performance time. The theater offers closed movie captioning and audio description for digital films that provide those features, with availability noted in listings. This is a useful Downtown option for viewers seeking international cinema or a special presentation. Across all three theaters, matching the film, projection format and access details to your plans matters more than simply choosing the nearest screen.
