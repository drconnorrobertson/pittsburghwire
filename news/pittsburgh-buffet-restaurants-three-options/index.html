Pittsburgh Buffets: 3 Places to Compare

Compare posted prices and meal formats at three Pittsburgh-area buffets, from Hokkaido’s seafood spread to Umiya’s Japanese all-you-can-eat menu and Golden Corral’s family-style selection.

By The Pittsburgh Wire October 10, 2026 3 min read

A buffet search can turn up very different meals: a seafood-focused spread, a Japanese all-you-can-eat menu, or a broad lineup of family favorites. These three Pittsburgh-area options were selected because their official pages publish practical details about menus, schedules or prices. The order is an editorial comparison, not a quality ranking or award. Confirm today’s service, final cost and any holiday changes directly before making the trip.

1. Hokkaido Seafood Buffet: most transparent posted pricing

Hokkaido Seafood Buffet is our lead editorial pick for diners who want to compare the bill before they arrive. Its official site lists weekday lunch from 11 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. at $16.99 and dinner from 3:30 p.m. to close at $23.99. Saturday brunch is listed at $17.99, with dinner at $23.99; Sunday is listed as all-day dinner at $23.99. The posted schedule says the buffet is at 4612 Browns Hill Road in Pittsburgh and gives regular opening hours as 11 a.m. to 9:30 p.m. Sunday through Thursday and until 10 p.m. Friday and Saturday.

For takeout, the site lists lunch at $11 per pound Monday through Saturday and dinner at $12 per pound; crab legs are separately priced at $19.99 per pound and are not part of the buffet price. Children between 36 and 55 inches are charged half price, according to the page. These are the restaurant’s currently posted figures; check whether tax, special items or a later menu update changes your total.

2. Umiya AYCE: Japanese dishes, hot pot and grill

At 1816 Murray Avenue in Squirrel Hill, Umiya AYCE describes an all-you-can-eat menu spanning sushi and sashimi, ramen, Japanese hot pot, charcoal-grilled dishes and teppanyaki. That range makes it a useful comparison for a group that wants to build a meal around different formats rather than a traditional American buffet line. The official page lists service windows of 11 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. and 4:30 p.m. to 10 p.m., plus the phone number 412-685-5151. It does not publish buffet prices in the information reviewed for this guide, so ask about current lunch and dinner rates, ordering limits and reservation availability before going.

3. Golden Corral: broad breakfast, lunch and dinner selection

Golden Corral’s Pittsburgh location at 900 Park Manor Boulevard in the Parkway Center area publishes day-by-day hours and separate breakfast, lunch and dinner menus on its official location page. The page lists 11 a.m. to 9 p.m. Monday through Friday and 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. on Saturday and Sunday. Breakfast service is described for weekend mornings; lunch items run Monday through Friday until 4 p.m., and the dinner buffet follows after 4 p.m. on weekdays. The menu spans made-to-order omelets and breakfast staples, then shifts to items including fried chicken, pot roast, fish, sides and desserts. Golden Corral notes that items vary by location, day and availability, so call 412-788-1776 about a particular dish.

What about Opal Grand Buffet?

Opal Grand Buffet is not included as an operating restaurant: as of October 10, its official website still says “coming soon” at The Waterfront in Homestead. Our separate Opal status report tracks its opening information and notes that the company has not published a confirmed opening date or buffet price on the checked official pages.

For a fair comparison, decide first whether you want posted meal pricing, a Japanese all-you-can-eat experience or a broad family menu. Then verify that day’s hours, taxes, child rates and food availability with the restaurant. Posted menus and prices can change, and an online listing should not replace a quick confirmation before a special trip.
