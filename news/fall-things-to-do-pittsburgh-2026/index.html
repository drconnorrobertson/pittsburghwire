Fall Things to Do in Pittsburgh: A 2026 Seasonal Guide

Foliage in the city parks, riverfront trails, football weekends, Phipps' fall flower show and more. Here is how to make the most of autumn in Pittsburgh.

By The Pittsburgh Wire Staff September 24, 2026 4 min read

Fall is one of the best seasons to be in Pittsburgh. The hillsides turn red, orange and gold, the rivers reflect the changing leaves, football takes over weekends, and the weather is usually cool enough to make walking the city's neighborhoods a pleasure. This guide covers the fall activities that come back year after year, so you can plan an autumn in Pittsburgh without guessing. Specific event dates change each year, so confirm details with organizers before you go.

See the Fall Foliage in the City Parks

Pittsburgh's large city parks are some of the best places in the region to see fall color without leaving town. Frick Park, which borders Squirrel Hill, Point Breeze and Regent Square, is the city's largest park and has miles of wooded trails through ravines and hillsides. Schenley Park in Oakland offers wide lawns, wooded trails and easy access from the universities. Highland Park and Riverview Park round out the list of big, leafy escapes. The Pittsburgh Parks Conservancy has maps and trail information for the major parks.

For a bigger view of the color, head to the overlooks on Mount Washington, where the hillsides framing the rivers put on a show. Our guide to the best Pittsburgh overlooks lists more vantage points.

Ride or Walk the Riverfront Trails

The Three Rivers Heritage Trail runs along the riverbanks through Downtown, the North Side, the South Side and beyond, and it is especially pleasant on a crisp fall day. It also connects to the Great Allegheny Passage, the long-distance rail trail that heads southeast from Pittsburgh toward Cumberland, Maryland. Even a short out-and-back ride from the city offers river views and changing leaves. Friends of the Riverfront is a good source for trail maps and access points.

Catch a Football Game

Fall in Pittsburgh means football. The Steelers play at Acrisure Stadium on the North Shore, and the University of Pittsburgh Panthers share the same stadium for their home games. Even without a ticket, game days transform the North Side, with tailgates, packed bars and black and gold everywhere. Local high school football on Friday nights is another longstanding tradition across the region.

Visit Phipps Conservatory

Phipps Conservatory and Botanical Gardens in Schenley Park stages seasonal flower shows throughout the year, and its fall show is a long-running tradition featuring autumn colors and displays of chrysanthemums and other seasonal plants. It is a good option on a rainy day. Check the Phipps website for the current show and hours.

Explore the Museums

Cooler weather is a good excuse to spend an afternoon indoors. The Carnegie Museums of Art and Natural History share a building in Oakland. The Andy Warhol Museum and the Mattress Factory are both on the North Side. The Senator John Heinz History Center, in the Strip District, tells the story of Western Pennsylvania, and its sports museum is a good pairing with a football weekend.

Shop and Eat in the Strip District

Saturday mornings in the Strip District are a fall ritual for many Pittsburghers. On game weekends, Penn Avenue fills with vendors selling Steelers gear alongside the neighborhood's long-running grocers, bakeries and specialty food shops. See our Strip District food guide for where to start.

Head Out for Apples and Pumpkins

A short drive from the city, farms across Allegheny, Butler, Washington and Westmoreland counties open for apple picking, pumpkin patches, corn mazes and hayrides each fall. Offerings and schedules vary from farm to farm and year to year, so check each farm's own website or social media for current details before driving out.

Enjoy Halloween in the Neighborhoods

Many Pittsburgh neighborhoods lean into Halloween with decorated porches, business district trick-or-treat events and costume parties. Walkable neighborhoods like Lawrenceville, Bloomfield and Shadyside are good places to see the season on display. Neighborhood business associations and community groups typically announce their plans in early fall.

Take a Walk Through Neighborhood Business Districts

Fall is the ideal weather for strolling the city's main streets. Butler Street in Lawrenceville, Walnut Street in Shadyside, Liberty Avenue in Bloomfield, Forbes and Murray avenues in Squirrel Hill and East Carson Street on the South Side all offer independent shops, coffee and restaurants within a few blocks. Our neighborhood guides, including Lawrenceville and Shadyside, have more ideas.

Tips for Fall in Pittsburgh

  • Layer up. Fall days can start chilly and warm up by afternoon. A light jacket goes a long way.
  • Watch the game schedule. Home football games bring heavy traffic to the North Side and Downtown. Plan around them or embrace them.
  • Peak color moves. Leaves change at different times depending on elevation and weather, so there is usually something to see for several weeks.
  • Confirm before you go. Event dates, hours and prices change every year. Check organizer websites for current information.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do the leaves change in Pittsburgh?

Fall color in the Pittsburgh area typically develops through October, with timing that shifts from year to year based on weather. Higher elevations and different tree species change at different times.

Where are the best places to see fall foliage in Pittsburgh?

Frick Park, Schenley Park, Highland Park, Riverview Park and the overlooks on Mount Washington are among the best spots inside the city. The Great Allegheny Passage offers longer rides through fall scenery.

What are good rainy day fall activities in Pittsburgh?

Phipps Conservatory, the Carnegie Museums in Oakland, the Andy Warhol Museum and the Heinz History Center are all good indoor options.

Where can I go apple picking near Pittsburgh?

Many farms in the counties surrounding Pittsburgh offer apple picking and pumpkin patches in the fall. Check individual farm websites for current offerings and schedules.
