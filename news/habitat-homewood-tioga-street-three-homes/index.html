Habitat Pittsburgh Announces Three New Homes on Tioga Street in Homewood

A Pittsburgh Land Bank partnership supports three first-time homeownership opportunities in Homewood, extending Habitat’s neighborhood work. Here is what the announcement confirms and how the broader program works.

By The Pittsburgh Wire October 9, 2026 3 min read

Habitat for Humanity of Greater Pittsburgh has announced a three-home affordable housing development in Homewood, adding another small cluster of first-time homeownership opportunities to the East End neighborhood. Its September 30 announcement identifies 7606 Tioga Street as the location for a planned groundbreaking ceremony and says the property was acquired through a partnership with the Pittsburgh Land Bank.

The announcement does not give a completion date or move-in schedule. It does establish the project's scale: three homes intended for three families. Habitat says it has already built and sold seven homes in Homewood, including three adjacent to this development. The new project therefore extends an existing neighborhood presence. The nonprofit, led by President and CEO Dr. Howard B. Slaughter Jr., is marking its 40th anniversary in 2026.

How the land partnership fits

The Tioga Street announcement follows another Habitat–Land Bank collaboration in nearby Larimer. A June 2025 release documented the completed sale of two vacant parcels for two single-family homes near Lincoln and Shetland avenues. That release described the property as Habitat Pittsburgh's first purchase from the Land Bank.

The Land Bank's stated role is returning vacant, abandoned and tax-delinquent property to productive use while supporting neighborhood goals, including affordable housing. In this partnership, land acquisition connects with Habitat's work financing and building homes for qualifying buyers. For residents following East End development, the two announcements show how separate, relatively small housing projects can share a common approach to getting land into residential use.

What Habitat homeownership involves

Habitat's explanation of its building model describes several parts working together. Donors and sponsors help fund tools and materials. Properties are purchased or donated, and volunteers work alongside participating families under construction supervisors. The organization says volunteers do not need previous building experience.

Future homeowners also complete financial literacy education and sweat equity work. Homes are sold through affordable, 30-year, no-interest mortgages, with mortgage payments supporting future Habitat Pittsburgh homeownership projects. That structure makes the organization both a builder and a continuing partner in the homeownership process. The practical commitment extends beyond the construction site to preparation for managing a household budget and maintaining a home.

Applications are currently closed

Readers interested in becoming homeowners should start with Habitat's current homeownership program page. As checked October 9, it says the organization is not accepting applications and directs interested households to an email list for future application windows. The Tioga Street announcement should therefore not be read as an invitation to apply immediately for one of these homes.

The program page describes eligibility requirements including first-time homebuyer status, a need for improved housing, living or working in Allegheny County, qualifying household income and a willingness to participate. It lists 350 volunteer hours and financial literacy classes as part of that commitment. Prospective buyers can review those requirements now, then watch the official page for an application opening. For Homewood neighbors, the next project-specific information to look for is a construction update and a confirmed completion schedule. Those updates will help distinguish the announced development from finished homes ready for their new owners.
