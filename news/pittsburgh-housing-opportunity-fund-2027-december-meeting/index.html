Pittsburgh’s 2027 Housing Fund Plan Proposes $2 Million for Housing Stabilization

See what the recommended plan sets aside for rental support and tenant legal aid, and how to follow the December 1 meeting.

By The Pittsburgh Wire Staff November 28, 2026 2 min read

Pittsburgh’s Housing Opportunity Fund advisory board meets December 1 as the city weighs its 2027 budget. The fund’s recommended allocation lists $2.04 million for housing stabilization and $1.57 million for tenant legal assistance. The mayor’s budget proposal lists $3 million for a broader housing and Urban Redevelopment Authority programming line. The two documents offer different ways to follow what the city might spend on housing next year.

What the housing fund recommends

The November 12 URA board packet lists $2,037,283 for the Housing Stabilization Program, with $1.3 million from the annual allocation and $737,283 from accumulated interest. Tenant legal assistance is listed at $1,567,141, including $1 million from the annual allocation and $567,141 from interest.

The packet also lists $6,817,063 for the Rental Gap Program, which supports affordable rental development. That amount combines annual funding, accumulated interest and loan repayments. The mix matters because a change in the city’s contribution can affect programs differently depending on how much they draw from each funding source.

The November packet records the advisory board’s recommendation and describes City Council review and approval as the final stage for the allocation plan. When an updated document appears, compare the figures by program and funding source. For renters, the most immediate lines to track are housing stabilization and tenant legal help. The Rental Gap Program is a separate item to watch for affordable housing development.

What the mayor’s budget shows

Page 74 of the mayor’s 2027 operating budget proposal lists $3 million for housing and URA programming under transfers. The line gives no program-by-program split. To see what will be available for any specific program, compare the adopted city budget with the final allocation plan and look for subsequent program announcements.

How to attend the December 1 meeting

The URA meeting page lists the advisory board meeting at 2 p.m. Pittsburgh time in the lower-level conference room at 412 Boulevard of the Allies, Downtown. The agency says residents can email HOF@ura.org for a Zoom link. To speak in person or online, register by 4 p.m. Monday, November 30 by emailing the fund or calling 412-255-6694. Speakers have up to three minutes. Residents can email written comments on agenda items and use the same address to request accessibility accommodations. Check the posted agenda and ask when the next allocation document will be available and whether it will identify the source of funding for each program.

Where to get help now

The URA maintains a Housing Stabilization Program page for current program information. For intake contacts and eviction help, see the Wire’s Pittsburgh rental assistance guide. To follow Council’s separate funding decision, see the December 10 city budget hearing and its participation instructions.
