Mill 19 Lands $10M Classified Defense Hub, Cementing Hazelwood Green's Role in National Security

RIDC and Parallax Advanced Research are building an 11,000-square-foot Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility at the landmark Hazelwood Green campus, funded by the Pentagon's Defense Innovation Unit and backed by more than a dozen committed tenants.

By The Pittsburgh Wire July 30, 2026 4 min read

Hazelwood Green has earned a reputation as one of Pittsburgh's most ambitious reinventions, transforming a former U.S. Steel site along the Monongahela River into a hub for robotics, advanced manufacturing, and university research. Now, a $10 million classified defense facility is about to add a new dimension to that story, one that positions the campus at the center of America's growing defense technology ecosystem.

The Regional Industrial Development Corporation of Southwestern Pennsylvania announced this month that it is partnering with Parallax Advanced Research, an Ohio-based nonprofit defense research institute, to build a Sensitive Compartmented Information Facility, known as a SCIF, inside Mill 19 at Hazelwood Green. The 11,000-square-foot multi-level space will be funded by the Defense Innovation Unit's Bridge Program and co-invested by the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania. Construction is expected to produce a facility unlike anything currently available to western Pennsylvania's rapidly expanding defense tech sector.

By the Numbers
$10M Total investment in the new classified facility at Mill 19
11,000 Square feet of secure space, including a SCIF and classified conference auditorium
12+ Companies and organizations already committed as tenants or users
2nd SCIF space in the Pittsburgh region, and nearly double the size of the first

A SCIF is a specially hardened facility that allows businesses and researchers to access, discuss, and store classified government information. Without one, a company simply cannot compete for most Department of Defense contracts, no matter how sophisticated its technology. Until recently, Pittsburgh businesses seeking that capability had to travel to facilities elsewhere in the country. That barrier, according to RIDC President Don Smith, was precisely what this project is designed to eliminate.

"In order to do business with the Department of Defense, you have to have access to a SCIF, and they're incredibly expensive to construct. It just became clear that there was an opportunity to do this at Mill 19, which is close to all these robotics companies."
Don Smith, President, RIDC

The facility will be more than a secure room. The planned build includes a classroom-style auditorium designed for classified briefings and conferences, making it a venue where government, industry, and academia can gather to coordinate on sensitive programs. That kind of infrastructure, rare in any mid-size American city, is what defense primes and federal agencies look for when deciding where to expand.

A Google Spinout Leads the Way

Among the first confirmed tenants is Aalyria Technologies, an advanced aerospace communications company whose technology traces its roots to Google and is already deployed across the U.S. Space Force, NASA, and allied military partners. Aalyria's Spacetime platform orchestrates communications networks across land, sea, air, and space in real time, a capability that sits at the cutting edge of modern military communications. The company signed its agreement to use the Mill 19 SCIF at the Pennsylvania Defense and Innovation Summit in Carlisle on July 15, where Senator Dave McCormick announced nearly $10 billion in cumulative defense investments tied to Pennsylvania companies and institutions.

For Aalyria, Pittsburgh's appeal goes beyond geography. CEO Brian Barritt, a co-founder of the company recently appointed to lead it, pointed to Carnegie Mellon University and the University of Pittsburgh as anchors that make western Pennsylvania one of the most compelling technology cities in the country. With the SCIF now in place, Aalyria says it can take on classified government programs from its Pittsburgh operation and hire locally for the engineering talent those programs demand. Voicd, a CMU-developed voice intelligence platform focused on detecting AI-generated deepfakes, is among the other tenants committed to the facility.

This new development makes Mill 19 the second SCIF location in the Pittsburgh region, following Walnut Capital's 6,000-square-foot secure innovation center at Bakery Square in East Liberty. But the Mill 19 facility is nearly double that size and adds the classified conferencing capability that a shared multi-tenant defense campus requires. Parallax subsidiary Fort Pitt Secure is involved in both projects, serving as the operational backbone connecting Pittsburgh's emerging classified infrastructure.

What It Means for Pittsburgh

For a city that has spent the last decade building a robotics and AI identity, the Mill 19 SCIF is an accelerant. Pittsburgh companies working in autonomous systems, advanced materials, space technology, and AI-driven platforms now have a local pathway into the defense procurement market that was previously out of reach. The Southwest Pennsylvania Defense Ecosystem coalition, spanning ten counties and launched at the same summit, exists specifically to channel that pipeline from research lab to warfighter. Mill 19 sits squarely at its center.

The investment also signals something broader about Hazelwood Green's trajectory. What began as a mixed-use development vision is evolving into one of the most strategically significant technology campuses east of the Alleghenies, home to Carnegie Mellon's robotics programs, advanced biomanufacturing through BioForge, and now a classified federal research hub. Pittsburgh has always been good at building things. The Mill 19 SCIF suggests it is also becoming very good at securing them.
