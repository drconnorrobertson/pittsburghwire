Selling Used Books in Pittsburgh: Cash, Credit and Copy Details

Amazing Books & Records and City Books publish different intake arrangements. Fungus Books adds a specialist shopping route where edition, condition and delivery terms deserve attention.

By Dr. Connor Robertson November 8, 2026 4 min read

Clearing a shelf and finding an unusual edition can both begin at a used bookstore, but they involve different decisions. Someone bringing books needs to know whether a shop buys, trades or accepts only selected material. Someone buying a collectible needs to identify the actual copy. Neither process is settled by seeing a business described as a used bookstore.

This guide uses operator information checked November 8, 2026 from Amazing Books & Records, City Books and Fungus Books. It does not appraise a collection or guarantee acceptance. Before contacting a shop, record the approximate quantity, broad subjects and condition. Photographs can make an initial enquiry clearer, especially for a larger collection.

Amazing Books & Records: read the buying instructions

The operator’s book-buying page describes a weekday noon-to-6 p.m. buying window and permits up to six boxes without an appointment, while asking people with more to call first. It lists preferences and exclusions, explains that it cannot take everything, and says some common material may receive only store credit. A buying window is not a guaranteed cash offer.

The two branches have different contacts: the Downtown shop is on Smithfield Street, while the Squirrel Hill shop is on Forbes Avenue. Ask the intended branch about the actual collection. If the books are numerous or unusually heavy, resolve the intake arrangement before loading a vehicle. Do not use the ordinary retail opening time as a substitute for the published buying window.

City Books: a credit enquiry starts before the boxes arrive

City Books’ FAQ describes trade-ins for store credit and asks people to email first. It may request photographs to assess interest and says not to leave books on the doorstep. That process differs from a promise to buy a household library for cash. Keep the desired outcome clear when deciding whether the route fits your needs.

If a shop declines some books, the remainder still needs a plan. Do not assume that an unsuccessful sale automatically becomes an accepted donation. Separate the books you hope to trade from material needing another destination, and wait for the recipient’s acceptance instructions. This is practical preparation, not a claim that a particular collection has been inspected or valued.

Shopping for an edition: compare the copy, not just the title

Fungus Books’ description identifies specialties including poetry, art, horror, science fiction and counterculture. That range can guide a search, but a subject specialty does not establish that the exact edition is available. Ask about the printing, binding, completeness and described wear when those details influence the purchase.

Its sale terms describe returns in the original purchased condition within 30 days after delivery and ordinary shipping on Monday or Tuesday. Read those terms for the actual purchase and ask about any expedited arrangement. A listing seen on a weekend is not evidence of same-day dispatch. Keep the item description so there is a clear record of which copy was ordered.

Separate reading value from collectible requirements

A reader who wants an affordable copy and a collector who needs a particular printing may accept different condition tradeoffs. State the requirement before comparing prices. Notes, inscriptions, missing jackets or a different binding can matter to one buyer and not another. This guide does not assign a market premium to those features or certify a listed book’s authenticity.

For new copies or a title not found on a shelf, the special-order guide compares other local routes. For a broader browse, the independent-bookstore guide introduces different neighborhoods. Keep the immediate task specific: identify a copy to buy, or obtain an intake decision for books you own. Those are useful questions a bookseller can answer without turning a general shop description into a guarantee.
