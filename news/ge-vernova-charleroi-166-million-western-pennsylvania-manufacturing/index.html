GE Vernova Commits $166 Million to Western Pennsylvania, Adding 700 Manufacturing Jobs

The global energy giant is expanding its Charleroi power transmission plant and investing across multiple Western Pennsylvania sites, betting big on the region's skilled workforce to power America's grid buildout.

By The Pittsburgh Wire
July 31, 2026
4 min read

Western Pennsylvania has long built the backbone of America and now it is being asked to power it. GE Vernova, the global energy equipment company spun out of General Electric in 2024, announced Wednesday a $138 million expansion of its Power Transmission manufacturing facility in Charleroi that will create approximately 275 new high-skilled jobs by 2028, with additional hiring planned well into 2030. Combined with parallel investments in Findlay Township and Rostraver Township, GE Vernova's total commitment to the region reaches $166 million and more than 700 new Pennsylvania jobs.

Governor Josh Shapiro attended the announcement at the Charleroi plant, where the company manufactures high-voltage circuit breakers, switchgear products, and instrument transformers. These are the unglamorous but essential hardware pieces that keep electricity flowing from power plants to homes, hospitals, and, increasingly, data centers. The timing is deliberate. Electricity demand in the United States is rising faster than at any point in decades, driven by artificial intelligence infrastructure and the electrification of transportation and industry. Companies racing to build grid capacity need these components, and right now lead times are stretched. GE Vernova is betting that a modernized, expanded Charleroi plant can help close that gap.

"Expanding our manufacturing capacity in Pennsylvania is a cornerstone of our strategy to meet the energy needs of an increasingly electrified world while helping modernize our nation's grid infrastructure," said Scott Strazik, CEO of GE Vernova. "By investing in American manufacturing and our workforce, we are strengthening the domestic supply chain and helping deliver the essential technologies our customers need to build a more reliable and resilient grid."

"We identified energy and manufacturing as two key pillars of opportunity. It's clear that strategy is paying off as companies like GE Vernova choose the Commonwealth as the place to expand and invest."
Governor Josh Shapiro, at the Charleroi announcement

What the Jobs Look Like

The new positions at the Charleroi facility are not abstract corporate headcount. GE Vernova has specified the roles it expects to fill: wiring technicians, mechanics, test technicians, logistics coordinators, and supply chain specialists. These are hands-on manufacturing positions that draw on exactly the kind of industrial training embedded in Western Pennsylvania's workforce pipeline. Washington County, where Charleroi sits, has long supplied skilled tradespeople to facilities along the Mon Valley corridor, and the new hiring represents a meaningful reinforcement of that tradition.

The state is providing support through a $5 million Redevelopment Assistance Capital Program grant and a $1 million WEDnetPA workforce training grant. GE Vernova is also eligible for additional tax savings through Pennsylvania's Manufacturing Tax Credit and the Qualified Manufacturing Innovation and Reinvestment Deduction programs, a full package that reflects how seriously Harrisburg is taking advanced manufacturing retention and growth.

A Broader National Play

The Charleroi expansion is one piece of GE Vernova's larger domestic manufacturing push. The company has committed to investing nearly $1.3 billion across its U.S. facilities and research centers between 2025 and 2028, a significant bet on American production at a moment when supply chain resilience and domestic content requirements have become strategic imperatives. Western Pennsylvania benefits disproportionately from this strategy: the region's combination of existing industrial infrastructure, a trained workforce, and proximity to major transmission corridors makes it a natural anchor for grid technology manufacturing.

For Pittsburgh and its surrounding communities, the GE Vernova announcement lands as further confirmation of a thesis that has gained momentum over the past several years: the region's industrial heritage is not a relic but a resource. The same geography and workforce culture that built steel mills and made equipment for a century of American industry is now positioned to build the infrastructure for the next century of American energy. Seven hundred new jobs in manufacturing, announced in a single day, is a significant proof of that point.

Quick Facts

GE Vernova was spun out of General Electric in April 2024 and is headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts. It employs approximately 85,000 people across roughly 100 countries. The Charleroi, Pennsylvania facility produces power transmission equipment including high-voltage circuit breakers and switchgear used in electrical substations worldwide.
