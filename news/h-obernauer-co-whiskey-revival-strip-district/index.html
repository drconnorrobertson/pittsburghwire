Historic Pittsburgh Whiskey Brand Revives in the Strip District

H. Obernauer & Co., a whiskey house founded by a German immigrant in 1885, is coming back to life more than a century after closing, with a tasting room planned for Smallman Street.

By James Coletta August 25, 2026 3 min read

Herman Obernauer built his whiskey business in Pittsburgh on a single promise: my word is as good as my bond. He put his name on every label he sold, a quiet guarantee of quality at a time when Pennsylvania was one of the country's dominant whiskey-producing states. That promise held for thirty years, until H. Obernauer & Co. closed its doors in 1915 and the name slipped out of the city's memory.

It stayed forgotten until Matthew Obernauer, Herman's great-great-grandson, went looking through his family's history and found it. Now he is bringing it back. H. Obernauer & Co. announced this month that it will reestablish itself in Pittsburgh's Strip District, with a tasting room planned for 2613 Smallman Street, the same stretch of warehouses and small producers that has become the city's most concentrated strip of food and drink makers.

A Name Restored, Not Reinvented

Matthew Obernauer has taken the title of chief revival officer, a role that is part historian, part founder. The company says it intends to source quality barrels and build blends that honor the original recipe philosophy rather than chase a trend, a deliberate contrast to the wave of celebrity-backed and private-label spirits that have crowded liquor store shelves in recent years. The pitch is simple: a name Pittsburgh can call its own, run by someone with a direct blood tie to the original maker.

"As a whiskey-maker, Herman was successful thanks to his dedication to quality, craftsmanship, and integrity. We're proud to revive his legacy and begin a new chapter for a name that still upholds those values."

Matthew Obernauer, Owner and Chief Revival Officer, H. Obernauer & Co.

The location carries its own weight. The Smallman Street space previously housed Kingfly Spirits, an upscale cocktail bar and distillery that closed abruptly in February after several years as one of the Strip District's signature nightlife draws. Its exit left a gap in a corridor that has otherwise been filling in fast, from produce stands and coffee roasters to newer restaurants drawn by the neighborhood's foot traffic and river-adjacent development. H. Obernauer & Co. steps into that space with a story built for it: a hometown name, a hometown address, and more than a century of dormancy to make up for.

What It Means for the Strip

Pennsylvania's whiskey history runs deep and, for decades, mostly untapped commercially outside a handful of big names. Pittsburgh's own contribution to that history has been resurfacing piece by piece, through small distillers and now through revivals like this one, where the appeal is not innovation but restoration. For the Strip District, it is another sign that the neighborhood's identity as a food and beverage destination keeps regenerating even when individual businesses do not survive. A storefront that closed under one name is set to reopen under one with a hundred-and-forty-year head start on a story.

H. Obernauer & Co. has not yet released a timeline for opening day, a full product lineup, or cocktail menu, saying only that details will follow in the coming months. For a city that has learned to expect its Strip District storefronts to turn over quickly, this one arrives with an unusual pitch: not something new, but something returned.
