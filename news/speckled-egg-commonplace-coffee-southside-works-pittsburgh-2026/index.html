Two Pittsburgh Food Favorites Unite at SouthSide Works in a One-of-a-Kind Collaboration

The Speckled Egg and Commonplace Coffee opened a shared 6,120-square-foot space on July 26, giving the South Side a brunch and coffee destination unlike anything else in the city.

By Claire Moreau July 30, 2026 4 min read

When Jacqueline and Nathan Schoedel opened The Speckled Egg in Downtown Pittsburgh's historic Union Trust Building, they weren't just launching a restaurant — they were planting a flag for the kind of neighborhood-first dining that Pittsburgh's food scene has long championed. On Saturday, July 26, the Schoedels raised that flag again, this time in the South Side, with a bold partnership that brings The Speckled Egg together with Commonplace Coffee inside a 6,120-square-foot space at SouthSide Works.

The two businesses have carved out a natural division of the sprawling venue at 428 S. 27th St. The Speckled Egg occupies roughly two-thirds of the space, bringing its full all-day menu alongside a weekend brunch lineup and a weeknight happy hour appetizer program. Commonplace Coffee takes the remaining third, offering a more relaxed counter-service setup with wall seating and community tables — a format drawn as much from the café tradition as from the kind of unhurried neighborhood culture both businesses have built their names on.

By the Numbers
6,120 Square feet of shared space at 428 S. 27th St., SouthSide Works
2nd Location for The Speckled Egg, which opened its Downtown flagship in the Union Trust Building
5th Pittsburgh-region location for Commonplace Coffee, which has grown from a single Indiana, PA shop since 2003

What makes the concept genuinely unusual is how deliberately the two businesses open toward each other rather than operating in parallel. Diners at The Speckled Egg can order Commonplace's specialty coffees tableside; Commonplace customers can grab a Speckled Egg breakfast sandwich on their way out the door. The combined footprint gives both brands enough room to move at their own pace while creating a layered experience that can start with a 7 a.m. pour-over and stretch comfortably to a weeknight appetizer and glass of wine.

From Smallman Galley to the South Side

The Schoedels met at culinary school at the Art Institute of Pittsburgh in 2010 and spent the better part of a decade working in regional kitchens before landing at Smallman Galley, the Strip District food-hall incubator that served as a launching pad for some of the city's most exciting young concepts. It was during a packed brunch service there that the idea for The Speckled Egg crystallized — a full-service restaurant organized around the egg in all its forms, elevated enough to surprise but grounded enough to feel like a regular. The Downtown flagship opened to a loyal following that has grown well beyond the weekday lunch crowd.

"The idea that started with a flat of beautiful, speckled eggs in the cooler has grown into something we couldn't have imagined."

Jacqueline Schoedel, Co-founder, The Speckled Egg

Commonplace Coffee carries an equally earnest origin. TJ Fairchild and his wife Julie drove a Volkswagen van to Indiana, Pennsylvania in 2003 to reopen a shuttered coffee shop on the edge of IUP's campus. The name, drawn from a Walt Whitman poem, announced the brand's priorities from the start: community, craft, and the conviction that a genuinely good cup of coffee should be an everyday experience. Over two decades, Fairchild grew the company into one of the most respected independent roasters in the region, with locations across Pittsburgh neighborhoods from Garfield and Squirrel Hill to the North Side.

A Neighborhood Ready for It

The choice to anchor in SouthSide Works is significant. Once the site of the Jones and Laughlin Steel Company, the 34-acre complex was redeveloped in the early 2000s into a live-work-play district that has matured steadily into one of Pittsburgh's most dependable mixed-use destinations. The addition of The Speckled Egg and Commonplace Coffee deepens an already strong dining and retail mix, and signals continued confidence in the district as a place worth making a special trip for — not just stopping by.

For the South Side as a whole, the opening adds a morning-to-evening anchor in a neighborhood that has long welcomed exactly this kind of locally rooted, owner-operated enterprise. The Speckled Egg is open Wednesday through Sunday; Commonplace Coffee opens seven days a week from 7 a.m. Together at 428 S. 27th St., they've created something the South Side hasn't had quite like this before — a single address where the coffee, the food, and the people behind both are worth knowing by name.
