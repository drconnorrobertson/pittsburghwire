Selling Records Around Pittsburgh: Prepare the Collection

Music To My Ear, Vinyl Remains, Amazing Books & Records and Jerry’s publish collection-enquiry routes. Identify formats, condition and the desired outcome before moving boxes.

By Dr. Connor Robertson November 8, 2026 4 min read

A record collection can include several formats, different pressings and a wide range of conditions. Before taking boxes to a Pittsburgh-area shop, identify what is there and whether the desired outcome is cash, trade credit or simply finding an appropriate recipient. A store buying records is not a promise to accept every item or pay the asking price seen for a different copy online.

This comparison uses operator information checked November 8, 2026 from Music To My Ear, Vinyl Remains, Amazing Books & Records and Jerry’s Records. No collection was inspected or appraised for this article. A brief list of artists, formats, quantity and obvious condition issues can make the first enquiry clearer.

Music To My Ear: describe the actual material

The shop’s used-record page describes reviewing records by condition, pressing and rarity and suggests sending details ahead when a list is available. It identifies the Babcock Boulevard store in Ross Township. Use that published route to discuss the collection before assuming a long trip or a large unloading will be useful.

A title alone may not identify the edition being offered. Keep sleeves and associated materials with the records they belong to, and describe missing or damaged parts honestly. This article does not prescribe a grading category or certify an edition. Let the buyer assess the actual copies and explain the proposed offer rather than equating a familiar album name with one fixed value.

Vinyl Remains: cash and credit answer different needs

Vinyl Remains’ official site invites record collections for cash or a higher amount in store credit and says it can discuss looking at a collection. That is an enquiry route, not a promise that every collection qualifies for a visit or a particular offer. Confirm the arrangement with the Mt. Lebanon shop before moving a large quantity.

If comparing cash with credit, identify whether there is something you actually want to buy from that shop. A larger credit figure is useful only in the context of its terms and your intended purchase. Ask what the offer covers and whether any items are being declined. Do not assume unwanted records can be left behind merely because other pieces are accepted.

Amazing Books & Records: use the record-buying instructions

The business’s record page describes cash and store-credit routes at its Squirrel Hill and Downtown branches, and enquiries about larger collections. These instructions concern records, while its book-buying page addresses another kind of material. A general used-book transaction should not be treated as a pre-agreed record appraisal.

Explain the quantity and location before asking about an off-site assessment. A statement that a buyer travels for collections does not establish that every household visit is available or that a particular collection is desirable. Confirm who is coming, what is being assessed and the expected process with the business directly.

Jerry’s: call about scale and format

Jerry’s buying page invites records and other physical media and asks people with larger collections to call about how to review them. It says a heads-up call is useful even for smaller quantities. This article reports that process without resolving conflicting address information found elsewhere during research; confirm the current drop-off location directly.

Different formats can need different handling. Identify LPs, singles, CDs or other media rather than calling every box vinyl. A shop’s interest in several formats does not establish that each has the same demand or proposed value. If the collection belongs to someone else, make the authority to sell clear before arranging a transaction.

Keep a record of the agreement

Confirm which items the shop accepts, the outcome agreed and how payment or credit will be provided. For ordinary browsing, the record-store guide covers another purpose. For instruments rather than recorded music, the used-gear guide explains separate intake questions. Neither a directory listing nor this comparison establishes an offer for an unseen collection.
