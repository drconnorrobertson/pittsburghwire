Grain & Berry Debuts First Pittsburgh-Area Location

The Florida-based healthy dining chain known for acai bowls and fresh smoothies opens its first Pittsburgh-area cafe in Pine Township this month, with local owners already eyeing a second site downtown.

By Rachel Osei September 14, 2026 2 min read

Pittsburgh's growing appetite for fast-casual health food is about to get a new address. Grain & Berry, a Florida-born cafe chain built around acai bowls, fresh-pressed juices and made-to-order smoothies, is opening its first Pittsburgh-area location this month in the Pine Tree Shoppes plaza in Pine Township, marking the brand's second foothold in Pennsylvania after a location near Scranton.

The North Hills cafe is owned and operated by local franchise partners Timir Surati, Vishal Patel and Roshan Patel, who bought the rights to bring the concept to the region and are already scouting a second Pittsburgh-area site, this one closer to the city center. It's the kind of bet that reflects a broader pattern in Pittsburgh's suburban retail corridors this year, where independent operators are increasingly the ones pulling national concepts into the market rather than waiting for corporate expansion teams to notice the region.

What's on the Menu

Grain & Berry's menu centers on build-your-own and signature acai bowls, including the flagship Grain & Berry Bowl, alongside smoothies like the Healthy Elvis, fresh juices, immunity shots, specialty toasts, wraps, sandwiches and salads. The format has been built for speed without abandoning the ingredient list a health-conscious customer expects, a combination that has carried the brand into more than three dozen locations nationwide since it launched in Florida in 2017.

To mark the opening, the Pine Township cafe is holding a grand opening celebration on September 19 from 9 a.m. to 9 p.m. The first 100 customers in line will receive free bowls for a full year, every guest after that gets a Golden Ticket scratch-off with prizes ranging from a $50 gift card to a discount on a future visit, and new customers can use a promo code for free first-party delivery during the store's first 30 days.

For Pine Township, the opening adds another fast-casual option to a retail corridor that has leaned increasingly toward wellness-minded concepts over the past few years, from juice bars to boutique fitness studios. For the three local owners, it's a first step in a plan that already reaches beyond the North Hills: a second Grain & Berry location closer to Pittsburgh's core is next on the list, a sign that the franchise partners see room for the concept to grow well past its opening day crowd.

That kind of local buy-in, an independent ownership group betting on a growing national brand and immediately planning its next location, is exactly the sort of steady, unglamorous investment that keeps Pittsburgh's retail map filling in. It won't make headlines the way a nine-figure development does, but multiply it across enough strip plazas and storefronts, and it adds up to a regional economy that keeps finding room for new ideas, and new ownership, to take root.
