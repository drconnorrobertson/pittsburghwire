Pittsburgh's Trades Built the Impossible: Parkway East Bridge Opens 8 Days Early

Hundreds of building trades workers completed the largest structure ever slid into place in U.S. history in just 17 days — a week ahead of schedule, under budget, and without a single recordable injury.

By James Carrothers July 29, 2026 4 min read

The Parkway East is open again — and Pittsburgh built its way back faster than anyone thought possible. On Monday evening, July 27, Gov. Josh Shapiro stood on the newly completed Commercial Street bridge spanning I-376 and announced that eastbound lanes were ready to receive traffic, with westbound to follow within hours. The project wrapped up eight full days ahead of the state's projected deadline, and it did so under budget and without a single recordable injury among the hundreds of workers who made it happen.

The feat earned a superlative that Pittsburghers can carry with pride: the new span is the largest known structure ever slid into place in the United States of America. The 825-foot, 22-million-pound bridge was constructed alongside the old deteriorating span, then pushed 102 feet into its permanent position using hydraulic jacks — a methodology known in engineering circles as an "accelerated bridge replacement" or a "slide-in" installation. PennDOT had estimated the entire process, from demolition of the old bridge through final striping, would require 25 days of full interstate closure. Crews delivered in 17.

"This bridge, this slide that took place, is the largest known structure to slide in place in the United States of America. And it's right here in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. We should be proud of that."

Gov. Josh Shapiro, July 27, 2026

The governor credited the building trades directly and without reservation. "I think our secret sauce in all of this is we have the best workers in the entire country in the trades who are able to do these big projects and get them done in ways that I think really marvel others who are watching," Shapiro said at the ribbon-cutting ceremony. PennDOT Secretary Mike Carroll echoed the sentiment: "It's what we do in Pittsburgh, in Philly, but truly in every county where this crisis occurs. We get to work."

By the Numbers
825 ft Length of the new Commercial Street bridge, the largest structure ever slid into place in the United States
22M lbs Weight of the bridge, pushed 102 feet into position using hydraulic jacks
17 days Total closure time — 8 days ahead of PennDOT's 25-day estimate
$100M Total value of the broader I-376 improvement project between the bridge and the Churchill exit, continuing through 2028

Seven Years in the Making

The project that looked, to commuters stuck in Parkway East traffic for years, like a sudden and dramatic transformation was in fact the result of seven years of planning. PennDOT District 11 executive Jason Zang acknowledged that what his team just delivered felt, from the inside, like crisis management at the highest level. "We've been operating in, literally, like an emergency mode," Zang said. "It was really no different than when we had the Route 30 slide years ago or when the Fern Hollow Bridge collapsed." That comparison — to the Fern Hollow collapse of 2022, which Pittsburgh also responded to with stunning speed — speaks to something deeper than project management. It speaks to a culture in this city of showing up and getting it done.

Contractors were incentivized with a $2 million early-completion bonus and faced financial penalties for finishing late, which likely added urgency to an already committed workforce. But Shapiro's remarks suggested the performance transcended any single financial incentive. This is Pittsburgh's second major accelerated bridge project under his administration — in 2023, after a tanker truck fire destroyed an I-95 overpass in Philadelphia, the state replaced the span in 12 days. The trades did that one, too.

What Comes Next for the Parkway

The reopening of the Commercial Street bridge is the most visible milestone in a much longer infrastructure story. PennDOT still has approximately two more years of work ahead between the new bridge and the Churchill exit as part of the larger $100 million I-376 improvement package. Cleanup and restoration of the site underneath the bridge are expected to stretch into next summer. The orange cones, in other words, aren't gone — but the Parkway East is open, and Pittsburgh's morning commute is moving again.

For a city that has spent years defining its future through technology, real estate development, and startup growth, the bridge project is a reminder of the workforce that has always been Pittsburgh's foundation. The building trades are still here, still building, and still — as it turns out — the best in the country.
