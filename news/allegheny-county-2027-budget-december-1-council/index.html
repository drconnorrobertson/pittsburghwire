Allegheny County’s 2027 Budget Is on Council’s December 1 Agenda

Council lists separate measures for operating spending, capital projects, grants and property taxes. Here’s how to follow each decision.

By The Pittsburgh Wire November 28, 2026 2 min read

Allegheny County Council’s December 1 agenda lists four measures that make up the county’s 2027 budget package. The meeting is scheduled for 5 p.m. Eastern. The proposed operating budget, capital budget, grants and special accounts budget, and county property tax levy each have their own file number. Checking those files individually will show which measures Council advances, amends or defers.

What the county executive proposed

County Executive Sara Innamorato’s October announcement put proposed operating spending at $1.25 billion, up 4.7%, and the proposed capital budget at $160 million. Her proposal included no county tax increase or county staff layoffs. The executive’s office said the operating plan included a one-time $10 million bond payment and $21 million in spending to draw down state funding for human services and juvenile probation.

The county’s fiscal plan page provides the operating budget, line-item detail, capital and special accounts documents and an interactive dashboard. If you are following a county service, look for the office responsible for it and the program line within that office. If you are tracking a road or bridge project, look in the capital proposal and match the project name and fiscal year. A funding line answers one question. A construction notice or project schedule answers when work will affect a trip.

The operating budget file shows a November amendment

As of November 28, the official record for operating resolution 14094-26 lists both the introduced document and an attachment labeled as amended on November 19. Its status is second reading, with the final-action field blank. The October figures above describe the executive’s proposal. The introduced resolution lists $1,250,397,037 in budgeted expenditures. The committee-amended resolution lists $1,251,267,037, an increase of $870,000. Check the version Council takes up and the recorded vote for the final amount.

Where the $870,000 increase comes from

  • Public Defender: up $535,000 for personnel and benefits, to $17,967,559.
  • District Attorney: up $535,000 for personnel and benefits, to $27,990,616.
  • County Council: up $200,000 for personnel, benefits and services, to $1,373,958.
  • Miscellaneous Agencies: up $100,000 for a new Turtle Creek Valley Council of Government line.
  • Children Initiatives: down $500,000 in its services line, to $3,168,808.

These changes reconcile to the net increase. See pages 3, 6, 10 and 11 in both versions of the resolution linked above.

Which measures to look up

  • 14093-26 would establish the county real estate tax levy.
  • 14094-26 would adopt the operating budget, including county expenses and debt charges.
  • 14095-26 would adopt the capital budget.
  • 14096-26 would adopt budgets for grants, special accounts and agency funds.

Open each item from the agenda and look at the attachments and version number. After the meeting, check the file history and minutes for the recorded action. If a measure has a new version, compare the relevant line with the October proposal before assuming the dollar amount or spending plan stayed the same. The county describes a process in which Council approves the budgets and the executive signs them.

Residents following a particular service should also check which government delivers it. Pennsylvania school districts, municipalities, and counties maintain separate budgets. The Wire also covers the December 10 Pittsburgh city budget hearing and the December 9 Pittsburgh Public Schools budget hearing.

County Council’s earlier motion listed two October public hearings and a November 6 deadline for written testimony on the county operating and capital proposals. The December 1 calendar entry is a regular Council meeting. To watch or attend, use the meeting details and agenda rather than relying on the dates in the earlier hearing motion.
