SCORE Pittsburgh Mentoring: Choose a Question and Prepare

Use SCORE Pittsburgh’s official mentoring route and make the first conversation practical.

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

SCORE Pittsburgh offers free business mentoring through volunteer mentors and provides a local workshop calendar. Its official chapter page links the matching process, and the mentor directory describes areas of experience. Use those tools to seek an appropriate conversation without assuming that a directory profile guarantees a particular person’s availability or a business result.

Define what you want to learn

Choose a question that reflects a decision you can actually influence. An owner considering a new sales channel might need help testing customer assumptions, while a founder may need to structure a business plan. Explain the decision and the current obstacle. A request that says only networking or success leaves a potential mentor with little useful context.

Use the official matching process

Start from the Pittsburgh chapter’s Find a Mentor route or its request form. Review the current options for contacting or matching with a mentor. The directory can help you understand available expertise, but do not treat a profile as a promise of an immediate appointment. If a requested subject requires specialized professional work, say so plainly in the enquiry.

Prepare an honest business summary

Write a short account of the business, customer and operating stage. Add relevant experience and identify what is still uncertain. For an early idea, separate what you know from what you hope will happen. You do not need to manufacture impressive figures to make a mentoring request useful; clear limitations help a mentor understand where to begin.

Make the meeting a working conversation

Bring a few questions and the specific information behind them. For a pricing discussion, explain the offer and the evidence you have about customer choices rather than asking for a price in isolation. Confirm the method and timing of the conversation. Send documents only through the agreed route and omit unnecessary account credentials or personal information.

Distinguish mentoring from an engagement

Mentoring can help you think through a problem, but it is not the same as hiring a professional to perform legal, tax, engineering or other regulated work. Clarify whether a suggestion is a research direction, an illustrative example or a task you should take to a specialist. Record your own next steps instead of converting a discussion into a guarantee.

Build continuity between conversations

After a session, summarize the assumptions to test and the action you will take. Suppose a retailer is exploring wholesale: the follow-up may be customer interviews and a rough capacity analysis. Bring the resulting evidence to the next conversation, including unfavorable findings. That makes mentoring an ongoing learning process rather than a series of disconnected requests for reassurance.

  • Specific decision or obstacle
  • Official Pittsburgh mentoring request
  • Business summary with uncertainties
  • Meeting questions and relevant evidence
  • Follow-up learning record
