Uptown Partners: Prepare a Business Directory Update

Use Uptown Partners’ published submission route with consistent public details and a maintainable process for later corrections.

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 21, 2026

A neighborhood directory can only help someone find a business when its public details match the operation. Uptown Partners provides a specific submission and update route for the Fifth and Forbes business community. Before using it, prepare a small record that someone on your team can maintain. The aim is a clear, verifiable listing, not a claim that a directory entry will create traffic or establish official approval.

Find the official submission route

The Uptown Partners business page checked November 21, 2026 links a form for adding or updating a directory entry. It also offers a business-support contact route. Use the form linked from that page rather than a copied, unverified form address. Confirm whether your location and business type fit the directory’s current scope. Submission is a request; it does not establish that a listing is accepted or that a particular publication date is promised.

Resolve basic identity differences first

Write the public trading name exactly as customers see it and keep any legal-entity explanation separate. Check the street number, suite and preferred arrival instructions against your own records. If the business operates by appointment or lacks a public walk-in location, say that plainly instead of supplying a convenient but misleading storefront address. Choose a public telephone and website that will remain available when a particular employee is away.

Make hours and categories useful

State ordinary hours separately from holiday changes, delivery hours and appointment windows. A general category should describe what a customer can obtain from the business, not every service you might offer someday. A concise description can explain a distinctive activity without unsupported superlatives. Avoid publishing credentials, accessibility features or service areas unless someone responsible has checked the exact statement. Keep temporary offers out of the evergreen description.

Worked example: an appointment-only studio

A fictional Uptown studio changes from walk-in retail to scheduled fittings. The update packet replaces the former open-door hours with an appointment statement, checks that the booking link works on a phone and identifies a public enquiry number. The owner includes a short change summary so a directory editor can see what needs replacing. After publication, the owner checks both the listing and the booking destination rather than assuming the submitted text was reproduced unchanged.

Assign ongoing correction responsibility

Save the submitted version, date and any acknowledgement in a shared business folder. Name someone to review the live entry after changes to hours, location or ownership. When correcting a record, identify the listing and the exact old and new text. Keep a change log so a second employee does not send a conflicting update. If a directory response is delayed, continue correcting your own website and customer messages through the channels you control.

Your next-step working file

  • Public trading name and verified customer-facing contact details.
  • Clear distinction between appointments, ordinary hours and exceptions.
  • One accurate category and a short factual description.
  • Submission copy, date and requested changes.
  • Named owner for live review and future corrections.
