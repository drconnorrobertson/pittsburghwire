Duquesne Student Consulting: Frame a Sponsor Project

Prepare a focused business challenge for a faculty-guided student consulting project at Duquesne.

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

Duquesne’s Palumbo-Donahue School of Business invites organizations to explore student consulting projects. Its official page describes faculty-guided coursework and typical deliverables such as recommendations and an executive summary. A prospective sponsor should frame a researchable challenge that fits an academic project, while confirming current availability and participation requirements directly.

Choose a bounded business challenge

Identify a question a team can investigate within an agreed project period. Examples might involve understanding a customer segment or comparing an operating process. Avoid presenting an entire company turnaround as one assignment. State what decision the organization expects to make and which parts of the work would remain with employees or outside professionals.

Separate analysis from implementation

The university describes student research, strategy and proposed solutions. Do not assume that a project includes ongoing system operation, legal work or implementation support after delivery. Write down the expected output and how the organization would use it. Ask the program to confirm what is realistic for the course and available student team before relying on it in a business schedule.

Prepare data that can be used responsibly

List the records needed for the challenge and whether they can be shared. Remove unnecessary personal or account information and discuss confidentiality through the university’s appropriate process. A sponsor should not promise unrestricted access before internal approval. If usable data does not exist, make that limitation visible when proposing the project.

Plan a sponsor’s participation

Identify a decision-maker who can answer questions, explain the business context and review interim work. Academic collaboration requires time from the organization, not merely a brief sent at the start. Ask about the proposed communication schedule and how scope questions will be resolved. Keep those responsibilities clear so the team receives consistent instructions.

Use current application information

The official page describes semester-related application timing and provides a contact for project partnerships. Confirm the current cycle and requirements directly. Do not infer that an enquiry guarantees a placement. Keep a separate contingency for the business decision if a suitable project cannot be arranged or the academic schedule does not match the organization’s deadline.

Evaluate the final work fairly

Suppose a team studies a potential new customer group. Review the evidence, methods and stated limits before deciding what to implement. Recommendations are not the same as measured commercial results. Retain the executive summary and presentation with the original scope, and record the sponsor’s decisions separately so future staff can distinguish student analysis from management commitments.

Prepare for your next step

  • Bounded researchable challenge
  • Expected output and implementation boundary
  • Approved data-sharing plan
  • Named sponsor and communication commitment
  • Current program contact and timing
