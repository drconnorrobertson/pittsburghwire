Neighborhood Allies HomeBase: Prepare a Specific Business-Support Request

Connect a proposed activity, budget and evidence before enquiring about HomeBase eligibility and current availability.

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

Neighborhood Allies describes HomeBase as support for small businesses through flexible microgrants for eligible activities. Its official page explains the current funding round and links additional business-learning resources. A published program description does not establish that your activity is eligible or that funding is reserved. Confirm the current criteria and application process directly before making commitments based on anticipated support.

Choose the business activity you want to discuss

Describe the proposed work and the operating problem it addresses. A business improving an online ordering process should explain the current process and the change it intends to make, rather than requesting a general grant for growth. Keep the activity specific enough that the program team can ask relevant questions. Separate work already completed from work being considered and record the dates accurately.

Check the current round instead of relying on an old announcement

Use the official HomeBase page and the linked current application information. Funding source, eligibility and availability can change. A past award story is not a promise that the same activity or business will qualify now. Record the date and source of the requirements you review. Where a criterion or timing rule is unclear, ask the program team instead of making an assumption that could affect a spending decision.

Build a supported activity budget

List the proposed items, services and associated costs with current quotes or clearly marked estimates. Explain what each item contributes to the activity. Keep requested support separate from other business funds and unresolved funding sources. Do not describe an expected award as cash already available. Ask which costs, dates and documentation the current program permits before relying on the budget as an eligible funding request.

Identify a practical result and how you would observe it

For the ordering example, the business could identify the workflow it wants to implement and the information it would track after launch. Avoid promising a revenue increase merely because a new system is planned. A useful result can be a documented operational improvement with a clear evidence source. Keep implementation, measurement and business outcomes distinguishable so the request does not overstate what the proposed activity can demonstrate.

Prepare only the information needed for the stage

Gather the business details and supporting documents requested by the current process, using the official submission route. Do not send passwords, customer records or unrelated sensitive material with an initial enquiry. If another adviser helps prepare the request, confirm who is authorized to submit and receive correspondence. Keep the final submitted version and any additional questions together for a consistent response.

Track the decision and its conditions

Record whether the request is an enquiry, submitted application, request for more information or confirmed decision. Any approval conditions and reporting requirements need to remain visible. If support is not available, retain the activity plan without presenting it as funded. HomeBase can be one resource to investigate, while the business still needs to make a realistic decision about its own costs, timing and responsibilities.

Prepare a focused next step

  • Specific proposed activity and current stage
  • Current eligibility and round information
  • Quotes and labeled budget estimates
  • Practical result and evidence source
  • Submission status and conditions
