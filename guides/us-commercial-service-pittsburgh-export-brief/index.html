U.S. Commercial Service Pittsburgh: Prepare an Export-Assistance Brief

Describe the product, market question and current export stage before contacting the Pittsburgh Commercial Service office.

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

The U.S. Commercial Service’s Pittsburgh page connects western Pennsylvania businesses with export-related information, events and local contacts. Its national services describe help with international strategy, market selection and potential business partners. Prepare the commercial question before requesting assistance. A service discussion does not establish export compliance, tax treatment, buyer reliability or a guaranteed sale; those issues need their appropriate separate review.

Describe the offer and current capability

Explain what the business sells, where it is made or delivered and what experience it has outside the United States. Identify which details are known and which are still being investigated. A Pittsburgh equipment supplier evaluating its first foreign distributor should distinguish an existing product from a proposed version. Keep technical specifications available for the appropriate conversation without placing confidential drawings or credentials into a general introductory email.

Choose a market question with boundaries

Identify the country or market being considered and the decision the business needs to make. If several markets are candidates, explain the criteria rather than requesting an unexplained ranking of every destination. Useful questions may concern finding information, understanding a route to market or evaluating potential partner searches. Keep assumptions about demand separate from evidence already collected, including the date and source of any prior research.

Separate commercial help from compliance work

List questions about the market alongside questions about export controls, contracts, tax, customs or payment risk, but do not treat them as one approval. Ask which service or professional should address each. A promising overseas inquiry does not establish that a transaction is permitted or commercially sound. Preserve the responsible reviewer and unresolved status for each topic so sales enthusiasm does not erase a required separate analysis.

Prepare a partner-search description

If a potential distributor or other business partner is relevant, describe the role sought and the capabilities that matter. Explain who would handle sales, service, inventory or customer support under the proposed arrangement. A name on a list is a starting point for due diligence, not proof of suitability. Ask what a proposed service includes and what the business must independently verify before making commitments or sharing sensitive information.

Request current scope and costs

Use the office’s published contact route to ask which services fit the stage of the project and what eligibility, timing or fees apply. Do not infer current terms from an old event page or another exporter’s story. Keep a written distinction between a requested service, an agreed scope and work completed. Include the business’s own preparation and follow-up responsibilities when planning the time required.

Build an export decision record

After the conversation, record the sources or services suggested, open questions and next action owners. For the equipment example, a market-research step may precede partner outreach, while a qualified compliance review follows its own timetable. Keep these dependencies visible. The brief should help the owner make a better-informed next decision without implying endorsement by the government or a promise of international revenue.

Prepare your evidence and next question

  • Offer and current export stage
  • Defined market question
  • Commercial and compliance issues separated
  • Partner-role criteria
  • Current service scope and follow-up
