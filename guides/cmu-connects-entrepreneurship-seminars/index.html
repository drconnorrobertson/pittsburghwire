CMU CONNECTS: Choose an Entrepreneurship Seminar

Use the Swartz Center’s published seminar schedule to connect learning with a real business question.

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

Carnegie Mellon University’s Swartz Center publishes the CONNECTS seminar series for people interested in innovation and entrepreneurship, with an emphasis on students. The current schedule describes individual sessions and registration. An entrepreneur can use the series as a learning resource without assuming that attending provides admission to another university program or access to restricted startup services.

Start with a learning objective

Identify one concept you need to understand more clearly, such as customer discovery, a business model or financial assumptions. Match that need to an actual session description. A broad interest in startups may be a reason to explore the calendar, but a specific question will make it easier to decide whether a session fits your current work.

Check the current event details

Use the official CONNECTS page for the date, format and registration route. The published series includes session-specific information, and those details should control planning. Confirm whether the event is upcoming, whether registration remains available and how attendance works. Do not assume that an archived presentation is a current live event or that one registration covers the entire series.

Prepare context without expecting private consulting

Write a short description of the business question for your own use. During a public session, avoid sharing confidential customer, financial or technical details merely to make a question sound concrete. Ask whether the material offers a framework relevant to your decision. A seminar is different from a confidential advisory engagement, and the presenter may not be able to evaluate your circumstances.

Translate the material into a small action

After a session, choose a practical exercise or research task. For a customer-discovery topic, that might mean drafting questions and reviewing them before conducting interviews. For a modeling topic, it might mean identifying unsupported assumptions. Retain the link to the actual material so you can distinguish the presenter’s framework from your own interpretation.

Keep university program boundaries clear

The Swartz Center offers several programs with their own participation criteria. Attendance at CONNECTS should not be described as acceptance into an incubator, funding program or university affiliation. If another resource interests you, read its official requirements and ask the appropriate contact. Keep any application or response in a separate record from seminar registration.

Use a learning log for the team

Suppose two colleagues attend a session on business models. Each can record one idea, one unresolved question and one experiment the business might try. Compare those notes and choose an action with an owner. This prevents educational attendance from becoming a vague accomplishment while also avoiding an unsupported claim that the university has validated the company’s strategy.

Prepare for your next step

  • Specific learning objective
  • Current session and registration details
  • Appropriate public-session questions
  • One practical follow-up exercise
  • Separate records for other program applications
