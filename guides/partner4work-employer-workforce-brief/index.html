Partner4Work: Prepare an Employer Workforce Brief

Frame a hiring, retention or training challenge before contacting Partner4Work’s Business Engagement Office.

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

Partner4Work’s Business Engagement Office describes support for employer hiring, retention, training and longer-term talent needs. Its current business page is a starting point for a conversation, not evidence that a particular company qualifies for funding. Prepare an account of the work, the workforce constraint and the result the business wants to understand. Keep recruitment, training and retention questions distinguishable.

Describe the actual work and constraint

List the roles involved, work location, schedule and tasks that are difficult to staff or develop. Explain whether the issue concerns too few applicants, a skills gap after hiring or employees leaving. These problems may coexist, but they need different evidence. A Pittsburgh manufacturer should identify the production task and supervision available rather than simply requesting more workers. Avoid including personal employee information that is unnecessary for the initial enquiry.

Bring a small baseline

Summarize the information the business already tracks, such as open roles, time to fill positions or completion of internal training. Define each measure and its period. An estimate is useful if it is labeled and its basis is visible. Do not build a complicated dashboard simply to make the request appear sophisticated. The initial purpose is to explain the problem well enough to ask which service or next conversation may fit.

Separate training needs from immediate vacancies

Describe what a person would need to learn, who could provide supervision and when training could occur. Identify work that must be covered while someone is learning. A course title alone does not establish that the training fits the job. If the business needs immediate coverage as well as development, show both timelines. Ask which options can be discussed rather than assuming one program solves every part of the staffing problem.

Treat funding as an unresolved eligibility question

If financial support is relevant, ask what current programs, eligibility conditions, approvals and documentation would apply. Do not promise reimbursement to an internal manager before receiving an applicable answer. Keep proposed costs separate from approved support and payments actually received. A published program description or a past participant’s experience does not establish this employer’s eligibility, a current award amount or permission to begin spending.

Assign the employer’s part of the work

Name the person who can clarify duties, the supervisor who can discuss training and the person authorized to review costs. Record the response expected from each. For a fictional employer considering a new evening shift, the brief should distinguish its planned shift from a confirmed launch and identify who can resolve scheduling questions. The workforce conversation is more useful when the business’s own commitments are explicit.

Record the agreed next step

After contact, document the service discussed, information requested, responsible people and next review date. Preserve conditions and unanswered questions in the same note. If the organization refers the business elsewhere, record the referral without treating it as an approval. Compare subsequent results with the original baseline using the same definitions. The brief supports coordination; it does not certify job quality, approve a funding request or guarantee a hire.

Prepare your evidence and next question

  • Role, location and schedule summary
  • Defined hiring or retention baseline
  • Training task and supervision needs
  • Unconfirmed funding questions
  • Named contact and next review date
