Pittsburgh Outdoor Dining: Build a Review Packet

Prepare the layout, ownership questions and operating responsibilities for a proposed outdoor dining area.

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

An outdoor dining proposal can involve furniture suppliers, designers, a building owner and city reviewers. Pittsburgh lists outdoor dining among its right-of-way programs. Start with a documented proposal and the current program guidance. The presence of tables outside another restaurant does not establish what is allowed at your address. This guide organizes the conversation; it does not supply an approved layout.

Identify the exact area

Mark the proposed dining area on a dated drawing and explain who controls the underlying space. Distinguish private property, a sidewalk and any other public area without guessing at the boundary. Record entrances, existing fixtures and neighboring uses that a qualified designer or reviewer should evaluate. Label measurements as preliminary until the responsible professional confirms them.

Ask which review applies

Use DOMI’s current right-of-way pages to locate the outdoor dining program and application guidance. Describe your actual proposal when asking about the process. A seasonal arrangement, permanent installation and changed layout may present different questions. Keep the official response with the drawing version that prompted it, rather than treating a general webpage as site-specific permission.

Separate furniture procurement from approval

Give prospective suppliers a provisional inventory with quantities, dimensions, storage needs and installation responsibilities. Identify anything dependent on review. Do not place an irreversible order on an assumption that a proposed arrangement will be accepted. Ask how substitutions, delivery changes and damaged items will be handled if the final approved plan differs from the initial sketch.

Plan operating responsibilities

Write down who sets out furniture, stores it, checks its condition and responds to concerns. Assign a person to maintain the issued documents and communicate any operating conditions to staff. A restaurant manager and a furniture installer need different instructions; neither should have to infer their duties from the other party’s invoice. Ask the appropriate reviewers about accessibility and other applicable requirements.

Review a practical change scenario

Suppose the original plan shows eight tables but the supplier proposes a larger replacement model. Compare that change with the documented layout before accepting delivery. Identify who will seek any necessary review and who bears the cost of delay. Keep the original proposal, revised drawing, written response and final order together so the team can explain what was actually authorized.

Check the purchase record against the layout

Record the model or dimensions of the furniture actually ordered, not only a catalogue picture. Include storage accessories and any separate installation work. When the order changes, compare it with the current reviewed proposal. Keep the supplier’s acknowledgement so the operator can establish whether a mismatch arose in the design, order or delivery stage.

Prepare this before asking for quotes

  • Dated drawing and property-control question
  • Current outdoor dining program guidance
  • Provisional furniture schedule
  • Named setup and storage responsibilities
  • Review record for substitutions
