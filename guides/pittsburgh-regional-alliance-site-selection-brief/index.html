Pittsburgh Regional Alliance: Prepare a Site-Selection Brief

Organize operational site criteria before requesting regional location and expansion assistance.

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

The Pittsburgh Regional Alliance describes regional assistance with business expansion and site selection, including real-estate and site-identification support. An owner considering Southwestern Pennsylvania can prepare the operational criteria behind a location decision before contacting the organization. A suggested property is not an assurance of zoning, utility capacity, environmental condition or suitability for a particular business.

Start with the operating requirement

Explain what will happen at the site, the activities involved and the expected scale. Distinguish office, storage, production and customer-facing space rather than using one total area figure. A company comparing a warehouse and a light-assembly location should describe loading, staffing and workflow needs separately. Keep present requirements distinct from possible future expansion so the initial search does not confuse mandatory features with desirable options.

Identify the geographic tradeoffs

Define the region or corridor under consideration and explain why it matters. Delivery routes, access to employees and relationships with existing facilities may influence the search. Avoid assuming that a familiar neighborhood is the only workable option. Document the tradeoffs management is willing to consider and the constraints it cannot change. That gives a regional contact a more useful brief than a request for the cheapest available building.

Classify must-haves and matters to investigate

Separate firm operating requirements from questions requiring technical verification. Utilities, access, permitting, physical condition and use restrictions may need specific authority or professional review. Do not treat an attractive listing description as that verification. Record the evidence required to resolve each issue and who can assess it. A simple criteria table should show confirmed facts, seller statements and unresolved assumptions in different columns.

Use one comparison basis for candidate sites

Ask for enough consistent information to compare the shortlist without hiding differences. For each property, record the source date, stated space, available documents and major unknowns. A lower quoted occupancy cost may omit a necessary fit-out or infrastructure change. Keep financial estimates labeled and request the appropriate separate review. The first shortlist is a research stage, not a recommendation to sign a lease or purchase agreement.

Plan a purposeful site visit

Before a visit, list the questions that can be observed and those that need documents or specialist input. Identify who should attend and which decision each person supports. For a warehouse example, the visit might clarify circulation while utility capacity remains a documented enquiry. Preserve photos and notes with permission and an identifiable date. Do not allow a visually appealing tour to substitute for required due diligence.

Keep commitments separate from exploration

Record next steps after the regional conversation and assign responsibility for unresolved site questions. A referral, introduction or possible incentive should remain conditional until its applicable process is complete. Revisit the brief when the operating plan changes so old assumptions do not carry into a new decision. The resulting file should make the search more transparent while leaving property, financing and legal commitments to the appropriate reviewed process.

Prepare your evidence and next question

  • Operating activity and space needs
  • Geographic constraints and tradeoffs
  • Must-haves versus unresolved checks
  • Comparable candidate evidence
  • Site-visit and follow-up owners
